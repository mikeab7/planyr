- Verify: sandbox — a layer COUNT is deterministic and identical in every run; it needs no noise floor, which is exactly why §7.3 called it worth chasing.

- **THE QUESTION, as B209568 left it.** *"304 layers against 118 is the largest deterministic difference between the two plans, it is identical across every raster arm, and nothing in this dispatch explains where it comes from."* Two data points support several readings, and the obvious one is that layer count tracks how much is in the scene.
- **WHAT THE REAL PLANS ADD.** Sylvestri is a **third data point from a real plan with 98 elements — roughly twice Bain's — and no raster overlay at all.** That is the observation that discriminates between "layers track scene size" and every other reading, and it is only available because B227472 landed a plan nobody could open.
- **(a) THE SCENE-SIZE READING IS REFUTED, and not marginally.** Bain (real) **320 layers / 316 Leaflet tiles / 47 elements / 598 canvas nodes** · Sylvestri **202 / 198 / 98 / 1,307** · Goose Creek **118 / 114 / 62 / 884**. Bain has the FEWEST elements of the three and the MOST layers; Sylvestri has **more than twice Bain's element count and 118 FEWER layers.** Stripping 159 canvas nodes out of Sylvestri moves the layer count by zero; hiding BOTH of Bain's rasters moves it by zero.
- **(b) WHAT IT ACTUALLY IS: the number of basemap tiles Leaflet holds in the DOM, plus four fixed layers.** Exact on every plan, in every arm, in every run, across two independent harnesses. That one identity explains everything B209568 recorded about this number and could not account for — why it was identical across all six raster arms (no raster arm touches the basemap), why it is identical across all five annotation arms, and why "304 against 118" read as the largest deterministic difference between two plans when it is a difference between two CAMERAS.
- **(c) ⛔ SO ONE CLAUSE OF B209568's FINDING (d) IS WITHDRAWN.** It reads *"roughly half is Bain's own scene: 1,035 canvas nodes against 884, and 304 compositor layers against 118."* The node count stands as a scene difference. **The layer count does not belong in that sentence** — it is a basemap-retention difference that would be there with an empty plan. The composite (+55.6%) and layerize (+46.4%) gaps it was offered as an explanation for are real, are unaffected, and are once again UNEXPLAINED.
- **(d) AND THE NUMBER ITSELF WAS MEASURED ON THE WRONG PLAN.** The real Bain reads **320 layers / 598 canvas nodes**, not 304 / 1,035 — the synthesised fixture was a larger, differently-shaped scene. 320 is the figure to quote, and the reason to quote it is that it is 316 tiles, not a plan.
- **WHAT THIS LEAVES OPEN, stated so it is not mistaken for closed.** *Why* Bain's camera retains 316 tiles where Goose Creek's retains 114. That is a tile-budget / overscan question about the initial fit and the retention policy (`lib/tileBudget.js`, `lib/tileLifecycle.js`), NOT a scene-complexity question — and it is the well-posed version of what §7.3 was asking for. Whether 316 retained tiles is itself a COST is a separate measurement nobody has taken.
