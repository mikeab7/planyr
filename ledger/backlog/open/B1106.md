- **The audited fact (docs/incidents/COLORADO-AUDIT.md §4).** Planyr measures in TWO feet frames and had never written that down. Drawn geometry lives in `mapLock.js` — a uniform scaling of spherical Mercator anchored at the site origin, so it is **ground-true at the site**. Screening distances go through the state-plane **grid** (`proximityScreen.js`, `coverage.js`, `fbcdWse.js`, `deedAlign.js`, `thoroughfare/ingestTransform.js`) and are treated as ground feet. So **yes, the app assumes grid equals ground wherever it measures on the grid.**
- **Correct by accident in Texas, wrong in Colorado.** Houston at ~50 ft: elevation factor 0.999998, about 0.01 ft/mile. Denver at ~5,280 ft: combined ≈ 0.99975, **~1.3 ft/mile, ~9 in across a 3,000-ft site**. A "within 1,000 ft" screen in Denver really measures ~1,000.25 ground ft — a bias, not noise.
- **Why it was NOT applied this session (the loud part).** A half-applied scale factor — some quantities corrected, others not — is strictly worse than none: the error stops being a known constant and becomes untraceable. B1112 therefore ships the honest reporting (`shared/coordinates/scaleFactor.js`: grid factor × elevation factor, what it is worth per mile and over any run, and `detectSurveyFrame` classifying a survey as grid / ground / other-scale / unknown) and applies nothing.
- **Scope when picked up:** carry a **project combined factor + project origin** on the site model (a persisted-schema change → additive field, bumped `SITE_MODEL_VERSION`, extended `migrate`), apply it consistently across every measuring path, and wire the deed/CAD import paths so a survey delivered in modified state plane reconciles. Front Range surveys commonly arrive in ground coordinates with the project factor printed on the sheet — read it rather than deriving it where it is available.
- **Needs its own golden master.** It touches every measuring path, so B1107's harness must be extended with a ground-frame section before the first line changes.
