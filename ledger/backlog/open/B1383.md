- **The owner's hypothesis, CONFIRMED on his geometry.** The connecting road's far end is anchored (a drive junction into a truck court), so the tee is the free end and **its position along the host is exactly what sets the road's absolute bearing**. Measured: the leg the tee governs sits **9.4° off the cardinal axis**; the cardinal is **18.4 ft south along the host** — and the B1382 dead zone was 25 ft. So the stuck tee was not merely inconvenient, it was the whole reason he could not get the bearing he wanted. Bearings here are TRUE bearings: the feet frame is axis-aligned to north (`mapLock.feetToLatLngPair`), no rotation.
- **AUDIT-FIRST, question 1 — was a bearing readout already there? NO.** Every `bearing`/`azimuth` in the workspace belongs to metes-and-bounds deed parsing or the deed basis-of-bearings check; nothing in the road tools reported one. So a few degrees off north was pure eyeball — exactly the frustration described. **Added:** a live bearing readout on the road-vertex drag, transient (drag only, so it costs the default view nothing — PANEL-BREVITY), reading degrees clockwise from north.
- **AUDIT-FIRST, question 2 — was a cardinal/angle snap already there? YES, and it was DEAD here.** Holding Shift while dragging a road vertex already locks the leg to 45° increments (`snap45` — due N/S/E/W plus the diagonals). On a junction drag it computed a locked point and the connect magnet then **overwrote it**, so the lock silently did nothing at exactly the moment it was wanted. **Made to apply** rather than newly invented: `cardinalTeePoint` composes the two — round the leg's bearing to the nearest increment, intersect that ray with the host, and slide the connection to the crossing, so the leg lands on an exact cardinal AND stays welded to the host. Releasing Shift is the override; Alt still bypasses connecting entirely.
- **The subtlety that made a first attempt refuse to fire:** the cardinal crossing routinely lands on a DIFFERENT leg of the host than the cursor is over (on his plan the crossing is one control point back from where his cursor was), so the ray is intersected against the host's WHOLE alignment and the crossing nearest the free point wins. The reported segment index travels with the point, because that index is what the tee is spliced into.
- **Scope held deliberately.** The owner asked to be able to place the road where he wants it; he did not ask for a bearing-constraint system. No perpendicular snap, no per-road bearing field, no constraint solver.
