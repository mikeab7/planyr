**⛔ THE TRIGGER, AND THE NUMBERS, STATED WITH THEIR REASONS (the dispatch asked for exactly this).** His own sentence is the specification — *"if I reload, it's immediately pretty quick and then a minute later or two, it's lagging just to go side to side"* — which describes a machine compared to ITSELF, a minute apart, not to a millisecond constant. So: **the baseline is the MEDIAN frame delta over the window from 5 s to 50 s after load** — skipping the boot, which nobody claims is fast (B1431 attributed four busy seconds there, and folding them in would set a bar nothing could clear) — and firing needs **three** conditions to hold at once over a **two-second** window of frames he is actually interacting through: **(a) LEVEL** — window mean ≥ **2.0×** baseline · **(b) SUSTAIN** — at least **half** the window's frames are individually that slow · **(c) FLOOR** — the window mean is at least **33 ms** in absolute terms.
- **2.0×** because this codebase's own measured run-to-run noise floor is ±6.3% (B1121 §1); a doubling is an order of magnitude clear of it, and it is roughly where a smooth gesture starts to read as a dragging one. Lower trades false alarms for sensitivity there is no budget for — three auto-captures per page load is the whole allowance.
- **two seconds** because it is long enough that no single stall of any cause can fill it and short enough that a bad drag is caught while it is happening.
- **half the frames**, not an average, is what makes it GC-immune: one 400 ms frame in a window of a hundred is one percent, not sixty. This is the dispatch's "not on a single slow frame", enforced rather than intended.
- **33 ms** stops a very fast machine reporting a doubling that is still imperceptible — twice as slow as eight milliseconds is a frame nobody can see. Self-relative alone would fire there.
- **MEDIAN, not mean, for the baseline:** a collection pause during calibration would inflate a mean and quietly raise the bar for the rest of the session — the failure mode where the instrument stops being able to see the problem.
- His display is pinned at **50 Hz**; an absolute threshold tuned to it would be wrong on every other machine, which is the second reason the whole thing is self-relative.

**⚠ A REAL DEFECT IN THAT DESIGN, FOUND BY ITS OWN TESTS AND FIXED BEFORE MERGE — worth recording because the shape recurs.** The sustain window was first qualified by a fixed FRAME COUNT (24 frames in two seconds). That makes the trigger **progressively less able to fire the worse the lag gets**: past about 80 ms a frame there are never 24 of them in a two-second window, so the sessions that most deserve a capture could not produce one at all. The window is qualified by the **TIME it spans** now (≥75% of the sustain period, with a floor of 8 frames): two seconds of evidence is two seconds of evidence whether it is 120 frames or 10. Both ends of the range are asserted — a 4 fps arm and a 144 Hz arm.

**THE MANUAL CONTROL — his perception is the one signal this programme has never had.** A small ◷ button in the planner's existing zoom stack (bottom-right, beside ＋ / － / ⤢), one click away while his hand is already there, deliberately not a menu dive. It captures the seconds BEFORE the press. Manual captures are marked `kind:"manual"` and stay distinguishable from auto-fired ones, because his report is more authoritative than the threshold. It confirms in place and, per LOUD-FAILURE, says so when it recorded nothing rather than looking identical to a success. **Placement is the cheapest thing here to move if he wants it elsewhere.**

**⛔ PRIVACY IS AN ALLOWLIST, NOT A HABIT, AND IT IS PROVED BEFORE EVERY SEND.** The payload is BUILT from `CAPTURE_NUMERIC_KEYS` / `CAPTURE_ENUM_KEYS` and then checked against them by `assertCaptureClean`, which the recorder calls before the row goes out and REFUSES the send on any violation (reporting the refusal). A denylist fails the first time somebody adds a field, which is how telemetry accidents happen. Counters, timings and view state only — no drawing geometry, no parcel or appraisal records, no owner names or addresses, no callout text, no raster bytes. The plan identifier is SANITISED rather than trusted: a plan id here can be a name he typed, so anything outside `[A-Za-z0-9_-]` is demoted to a short non-reversible hash (`planIdKind` says which), and attribution strings are stripped of query and path (a URL in this app can carry a plan id). The test drives a deliberately DIRTY fixture — his name, a street address, a callout string, a polygon, a parcel id, raster bytes — and asserts none of it survives anywhere in the encoded row. Same discipline `scripts/extract-plan.mjs` already follows.

**PERSISTENCE — readable with no action from him, and in the RIGHT TIER.** The row travels on the EXISTING `public.client_errors` table via `reportClientEvent("perfcap", …)` — the precedent the dispatch named, and the B468 pattern that needs **no schema migration and no SQL step from him**. That column truncates at 2,000 characters, so the frame track is packed one base-64 character per frame with the rare slow frames carried explicitly (the clamp can never hide a stall) and the encoder **trims oldest-first until it fits, recording what it dropped** — a capture that silently lost its tail would read as a calmer episode than the one that happened. Bounded at ≤ 9 rows per page (3 auto + 6 manual), far under `clientErrors`' shared 100-row per-page ceiling, because a recorder that spends the error channel's budget is a worse regression than any data it collects is worth. The **FULL, untrimmed** capture is kept on the device — **IndexedDB, the LARGE tier, per TIER-BY-REBUILDABILITY**: a diagnostic must never compete with saved plans for the ~5 MB store that was measured 78% full on his machine. Pruned to three on every write, declared `rebuild: NONE / reclaimable: false` (nothing can rebuild a capture — the moment is gone), and **surfaced in the plan menu → Storage with its own Delete control**, so the class can never grow without being visible.

**⛔ GUARD 1 — OVERHEAD, MEASURED, NOT CLAIMED, AND THE FIRST TWO ATTEMPTS AT MEASURING IT WERE WRONG.** `ui-audit/verify-perf-recorder.mjs` (`npm run perf:recorder`, hermetic — no external host, no sign-in, no plan fixture, so it is in CI's reach rather than filed as a live check):
- **TIME: 0.07 µs per frame** against a stated **2.0 µs** bound chosen before the run — 0.0004% of a 16.7 ms frame budget.
- **END TO END: 0 ms** difference in median frame time between recorder ON and OFF, over three interleaved reps of an identical scripted gesture (`?perfrec=off` is both the OFF arm and a real field kill switch; a page with it set never even downloads the recorder chunk).
- **ALLOCATION: guarded STRUCTURALLY, and that decision is a measurement rather than a preference.** Planting a real per-frame object plus a per-frame string in the hot path moved the timing bench from 0.05 to 0.07 µs/frame — nowhere near its bound — because a young-generation bump allocation in a tight loop is about twenty nanoseconds. A heap-delta arm over `performance.memory` was then built **and removed**: without `--enable-precise-memory-info` the value is quantised to 100 KB and cached for twenty minutes (the planted defect read exactly **0** on every run), and with the flag ambient growth only ADDS while a scavenge only SUBTRACTS — so the MAX read **33.6 bytes/frame on a hot path that allocates nothing** and the MIN read **−21.5 on one that allocates ~48**. Both statistics measured, both wrong, and no third rescues a signal that noisy. **A guard that cannot fail on its own defect is decoration**, so it was replaced by a deterministic source rule over the per-frame path (`onFrame` · `pushFrame` · `pushTask` · `pushCounters` · `feedFrame` · `pushWindow` · `evictWindow` · `windowSpan`): no object or array literal, no template literal, no constructor, no spread, no allocating array method, no arrow function. The fire branch was split out of `feedFrame` for exactly this reason — it is the only allocating part and it runs at most three times per page.
- **Bundle cost, paid where it belongs:** the recorder is a **16.6 KB LAZY chunk** fetched in an idle gap after boot (never on any route's critical path — the bundle audit's route closure is over static imports), plus **3.1 KB** on the site route for the control and the handle. `siteRouteChunks` still 7 of 7 and the allowlist clean — the first cut broke both, because a module reachable from a lazy chunk and the site route but NOT the entry gets hoisted into a third chunk that lands on a plain Site load; the handle is imported from `main.jsx` to put it in the entry every route already downloads. `bundle.totalJsBytes` was RATCHETED (4599.1 → 4693.4 KB) with the reason on the record: main was already 73.6 KB above that stale baseline before this branch and 20.6 KB of new lazy code carried it over.

**⛔ GUARD 2 — ANTI-ROT, AND IT IS MUTATION-PROVEN IN BOTH DIRECTIONS.** A recorder that never fires is indistinguishable from a healthy app — the exact failure `count-pond-invocations --assert` and the decode/annotation fault arms exist to close. The unit half is mutation-proven too: the planted per-frame object turns the allocation rule red, and renaming a hot-path function turns the NOT-OBSERVING check red rather than leaving it trivially green.
