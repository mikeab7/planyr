- **The instrument.** `ui-audit/diagnose-dock-leaf-fold.mjs` — the same fold applied as a page-side DOM mutation on ONE build (so a whole zoom ladder is cheap), screenshotted and pixel-diffed with the existing `lib/pngDiff.mjs`, at the same bar: byte-identical or 1/255 on one channel. It measures THREE renderings of the same 432 leaves instead of two, which is what made the cause testable:
  **A** the shipped `<rect>`s · **B** N `<path>`s, one rectangle subpath each (same count, same order, same opacity — only the primitive changes) · **C** ONE `<path>` with N subpaths (the collapse itself).
- **REFUTED — the semi-transparent fill is INNOCENT.** Fold both arms with `fill-opacity` forced to 1 and the difference does not move: at ppf 3, **12/255 over 8,446 px transparent vs 12/255 over 8,453 px opaque**. Every zoom rung behaves the same way. The compositing story — two leaves' antialiased edges sharing pixel coverage — was a plausible mechanism that is not the mechanism.
- **WHAT IT ACTUALLY IS: the PRIMITIVE.** `<rect>` → `<path>` is already not identical *before anything is folded*: **12–23/255 on 0.02–0.66% of canvas pixels at EVERY rung**, including ppf 3, where a leaf is 24 px wide with hundreds of px of clear space around it and no compositing interaction is possible. Chromium does not rasterise a rect and a rectangular path to the same antialiased edge. The fold itself (B→C) adds a further 4–44/255 on top.
- **THEREFORE THERE IS NO GATE THAT SAVES IT.** The zoom-band idea this retry set out to test — collapse only where neighbouring leaves provably cannot share a pixel — is dead on arrival, because the delta is present exactly where they cannot. **0 of 6 rungs meet the bar** on any of the three comparisons. The stall dividers fold exactly because they are stroked `<line>`s becoming stroked subpaths: the same rasteriser strategy on both sides of the change. There is no such representation for a filled, outlined rectangle.
