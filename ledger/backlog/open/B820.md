- **THE CAUSE.** `arrangePeers` scopes the peer set to same-family-same-band (`markups.filter(m => (m.behindEls === true) === (t.behindEls === true))`), so the command reaches the bottom of the MARKUP layer and `arrangeFlags` then correctly reports `atBottom` — of a band the user cannot see. **This is a LOUD-FAILURE violation of the worst kind: not silence, but a confident report of success for a DIFFERENT operation than the one requested.**
- **⛔ THE MEASUREMENT HALF OF THE REPORT DID NOT REPRODUCE, and the design must not be built on it.** It was reported that an area measurement drawn over an opaque markup "vanished completely" and that nothing can bring it forward. Driven on the same plan with TWO deliberately opaque markups over a building: the family paint order back-to-front is **`parcel → el → markup → callout → measure`**, so a measurement is TOPMOST of the four by construction, and its outline, its edge labels and its acreage chip all render clearly over both markups (screenshot in the run). A measurement therefore cannot fall behind a markup by default, and its cross-band toggle is a REVERSIBLE pair ("Send behind the plan" / "Bring above the plan"), so a forward command does exist — it is simply not offered while the measurement is already in front. **The one state that would produce the reported symptom is a measurement previously sent behind the plan; that is an open question with the owner and must not be guessed.**
- **⛔ CONFIRMED AND SEPARATE: ONE CONCEPT, TWO VOCABULARIES.** The markup menu says **"Send behind buildings"**; the measurement and callout menus say **"Send behind the plan"**. Both observed in the same run, minutes apart. This is exactly the parity drift **B293072** was built to kill, and it survived because `e2e/elementCapabilities.table.js` declares `crossBand: "yes"` for every type — a cell that any escape hatch satisfies REGARDLESS of what it is called or which direction it travels. The table needs to declare the cross-band CONTRACT (name + both directions), not merely its existence.
- **⚠ FOUND WHILE DRIVING, not in the report: a measurement's LABEL CHIP eats presses aimed at a markup underneath it.** The chip is far wider than the measurement itself and answers `elementsFromPoint` well outside it; a right-click aimed at a markup opened the MEASUREMENT's menu. That is CHROME-NEVER-EATS-A-PRESS in the wild and is adjacent to the reported "cannot right-click a markup back" case.
- **⛔ NOT YET FIXED, deliberately.** The brief's design instruction is to write the ordering CONTRACT across every pair of types as committed data and render the menus from it — and one of the three band-pair behaviours it was to be built around does not exist. Designing the contract around a refuted premise is the exact failure this item is made of. **Blocked on one owner question:** should a measurement ALWAYS outrank decoration, or may it be tucked under a callout deliberately? The contract is a one-line change either way, which is why it is worth waiting for rather than guessing.
