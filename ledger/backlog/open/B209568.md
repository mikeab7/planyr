- **THE MISS, and the owner was right about it.** Every number this speed program has produced came from Goose Creek or a scene derived from it. He has reported TWO sites as slow — Bain and Sylvestri — and the harness could open neither. So the instrument has been measuring the one site it CAN open while he reports on two it cannot, and calling the result a floor. **`ui-audit/fixtures/bain-concept-a.json`** ends that: 53 elements in his exact measured kind counts (building 12 · sidewalk 12 · parking 12 · road 8 · paving 6 · trailer 2 · pond 1), 5 parcels, and **both rasters at their exact measured dimensions, opacities, `ftPerPx` and IndexedDB-string storage path** — 1728 × 2592 @ 0.55 over 1800 × 1167 @ 1.0. Settings/origin/county are held EQUAL to the Goose Creek control so the comparison isolates rasters instead of confounding them.
- **THE INSTRUMENT THE PROGRAM DID NOT HAVE, and its absence is why this could not have been found before.** Every cost metric here is `ScriptDuration + LayoutDuration + RecalcStyleDuration`. All three are main-thread work that happens BEFORE a pixel exists, so **none of them can see paint, raster, image decode or compositing** — which is exactly where a large translucent overlay spends its time. `ui-audit/lib/rasterCost.mjs` adds that half from Chromium's own tracing, bucketed by name and differenced across the same viewport-neutral pan.
- **THE STATISTICS, because the verdicts below depend on them and the obvious one does not work here.** Two independent batteries (4 reps + 6 reps, **60 runs, 0 suppressed**), arms INTERLEAVED. The repo's usual noise floor is a RANGE — `(max − min) / median` — which grows monotonically with n, so one contaminated rep set it (±48.4% at 4 reps) and no amount of extra data could ever tighten it. **The floor is reported unchanged and clears nothing**; what is ADDED is the analysis the interleaved design already supported and nobody was reading — a **paired sign test rep-for-rep**, which cancels a slow machine-minute because it hits both arms together. Bar declared in code ahead of any result (p ≤ 0.05); six paired reps is the arithmetic minimum it can speak at.
- **(a) SEMI-TRANSPARENCY IS NOT THE MECHANISM — the hypothesis named first is dead.** Forcing the overlay to opacity 1.0 (same pixels, same footprint, alpha removed) separates on **NOTHING**: paint 5/10, raster 6/10, composite 7/10, render 8/10 — not one bucket reaches p ≤ 0.05.
- **(b) NOR IS PIXEL COUNT. The cost tracks the AREA THE OVERLAY COVERS, not its resolution.** `quarter` cuts both rasters to ¼ the pixels **while holding the on-map footprint exactly** — decoded texture 17.1 → 4.3 MB, a 75% cut — and raster work falls **6.3%** (9/10, p=0.021). Removing the overlay outright falls **24.3%** (9/10, p=0.021). Three quarters of the texture buys a quarter of the saving.
- **(c) BECAUSE THE OVERLAY NEVER GETS ITS OWN COMPOSITOR LAYER — and this one count explains (a) and (b) together.** **304 layers with it · 304 at quarter size · 304 opaque · 304 hidden · 304 with both rasters gone.** Identical in every arm of every run. It is painted into the MAIN CONTENT LAYER, so it is re-rastered as part of that layer's tiles at SCREEN scale over the area it covers: alpha is free because the blend rides a raster pass that was going to run anyway, and source resolution is nearly free because the raster is at screen scale, not image scale.
- **(d) AND MOST OF BAIN'S COST IS NOT THE RASTERS AT ALL.** Bain's render work is **40% above** Goose Creek's (9/10, p=0.021). **Strip BOTH rasters out of Bain and it is still 20.9% above** (9/10, p=0.021), with **composite +55.6% and layerize +46.4%, both at 10/10 (p=0.002)**. Roughly half the gap is rasters and roughly half is Bain's own scene — 1,035 canvas nodes against 884, and **304 compositor layers against 118**. Nothing here explains where 304 layers come from; it is the largest deterministic difference between the two plans and the next thing to measure.
- **(e) MAIN-THREAD WORK IS IDENTICAL ON THE TWO PLANS — which is the whole story of the miss in one line.** Script + layout + style: **5 of 10 paired reps, p = 1.000.** Bain 795 ms, Goose Creek 790 ms. **The only cost metric this program owns cannot tell the two plans apart.** Every difference above lives in paint, raster, composite and layerize, and the un-quantised work figure is structurally blind to all four.
