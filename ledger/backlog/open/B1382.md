- **The mechanism, measured on his rows rather than inferred.** Of the four candidate causes in the brief it is the fourth, produced by the first: **the junction re-solves on release and reverts.** A tee is stored as a control point spliced into the HOST road's alignment, and `planRoadConnect` routed every tee — including one that already existed — through `insertRoadVertex`'s reuse rule. That rule's tolerance scales with the host's width, and his host (`e1454717dshobp`) is a 100 ft aisle, so it is **25 ft**. The endpoint being dragged was already welded to that node, so any drop within 25 ft "reused" it and the endpoint was welded straight back to where it started. **The ghost tracked the cursor the entire drag**, which is why it reads as "stuck" rather than as "nothing is grabbable". Past 25 ft it did move — and spliced a SECOND node in, leaving the original behind (host 9 pts → 10).
- **The correction he wants is ~18 ft. The dead zone was 25 ft.** So his adjustment did literally nothing, every attempt.
- **A SECOND stuck mechanism, found while reproducing and fixed with it.** `findRoadConnect` ranks candidates by distance to the pavement EDGE (`max(0, d − halfW)`, B961), which **saturates**: on a 100 ft road that is 0 for every point within ~50 ft of the centerline, so a strict `<` comparison resolved a 50 ft-wide band of ties by SCAN ORDER, not proximity. Sliding along a wide host that way kept resolving to the first segment scanned — whose clamped projection is the shared vertex itself. `dist` stays edge-relative (B961's cross-target fairness is untouched); ties now break on the raw centerline distance, which does not saturate.
- **Fix.** New pure `teeNodeIndex` (which host control point is this endpoint already welded to — interior only; an endpoint meet is a WELD and must not move the host's extent) + `slideTeeNode` (drop the held node, re-project onto what is left so a slide PAST a neighbour re-sequences instead of folding the alignment back on itself, splice it in at its new home). `planRoadConnect` takes `opts.fromPt` — the endpoint's pre-drag coordinate — and `startRoadVtx` stashes it. The reuse tolerance on a SLIDE is the tight `ROAD_VERTEX_COLLAPSE_FT`, never the width-scaled one: a slide is a deliberate repositioning of a node that already exists, so the only thing worth collapsing onto is a true coincidence.
- **Result on his geometry:** the tee slides continuously in both directions and stays where it is dropped, at every distance tested from 1 ft to 80 ft; the host keeps its 9 control points at every one of them (the debris path is gone, not just the dead zone).
