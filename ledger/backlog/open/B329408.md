- **THE STATE.** On the `main` push builds for `81f7725`, `1c41514` and `80fb475` (the last being B326416's merge), every step passes — lint, all five drift guards, the mint gate, the tests, the build — and **only** step 16, *Performance budget*, fails. Four metrics are over: `siteRouteJsBytes`, `notesRouteJsBytes`, `totalJsBytes`, `largestChunkBytes`.
- **IT IS NOT A REGRESSION FROM ANY ONE BRANCH, AND THE AUDIT SAYS SO ITSELF.** B266084's attribution line on the `1c41514` run reads: *"4 metric(s) arrive ABOVE BASELINE before this branch adds a line — that drift belongs to main, not to this pull request."* At that point main carried **+58.0 KB** (site route), **+33.9 KB** (notes), **+196.0 KB** (total) and **+53.8 KB** (largest chunk) above their baselines. It is accumulated drift across many merges.
- **WHY IT LOOKS INVISIBLE AND KEEPS GETTING MERGED PAST.** A `pull_request` run judges a branch RELATIVE to its base ref, so a PR adding a few KB is green; a push to `main` is judged **ABSOLUTELY**. That asymmetry is deliberate and documented in the audit's own output (*"main cannot drift past its own band without going red there"*) — it is the design working. The consequence nobody owns is that **every PR can be green while `main` is permanently red**, so the signal has stopped meaning anything and the next session will assume it is background noise.
- **WHAT THIS ITEM IS NOT.** It is not a licence to ratchet four baselines to make the red go away. Raising a baseline is a product decision about download weight and needs the same justification as any other; doing it to silence accumulated drift is exactly what `test/perfBudgetPolicy.test.js` exists to prevent. The two honest paths are **optimize back inside the band** (B1064 owns the real Site-route gap) or **ratchet with a stated reason per metric** — and someone has to choose, per metric.
- **STOPPING RULE (STANDING RULE #2 clause 5):** closes when a `main` push build goes green on step 16 — either by optimization or by a per-metric ratchet with its reason recorded in `ratchetLog`. If neither is judged worth doing, the item closes by an explicit owner decision to raise the band, recorded on the item — never by the check being ignored into permanence.
