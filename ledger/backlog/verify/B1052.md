- **Measured on his LIVE geometry, not inferred.** Auditing every interior vertex against the chord between its own neighbours: the 40′ truck loop `e38duuwgj` carries **ten** vertices sitting **0.00–0.25 ft off** that chord, and the 40′ aisle `e1454683splyoj` four more. They bend the alignment by nothing. Across his plan, **36 control points of which 16 do no work.**
- **Where they come from.** Every road-to-road connect SPLICES a vertex into the target road at the tee point (`insertRoadVertex` via `planRoadConnect`) and **no path ever removes one**. `insertRoadVertex`'s `collapseFt` only reuses a vertex when the new point lands near THAT SEGMENT'S endpoint — after the first insert, the next connect lands on a different segment and always splices a fresh one. Redraw the side road, drag its end, reconnect a foot over: each attempt leaves its own grip behind.
- **Why B1008 and B1010 didn't catch it — and this is the useful part.** Both attacked vertex debris and both ruled out THIS mode *by design*: B1008 collapses vertices within ~1.5 ft of one another; B1010 adds "a short stub the alignment TURNS through" and states outright that a COLLINEAR stub is harmless because it distorts no geometry. It doesn't distort anything — and it is still clutter the user never authored, which is the actual complaint. The lesson: "does it change the geometry" was the wrong acceptance test; "did the user put it there, and does it do anything" is the right one.
- **Fix:** new pure `simplifyRoadVertices(pts, vtx, tolFt, { pinned, pinTolFt, reference })`. Greedy — remove the least-contributing interior vertex, then **re-measure EVERY ORIGINAL point against the simplified polyline** and stop the moment any would sit more than `tolFt` (1.5 ft) off it. Measuring against the ORIGINAL rather than the previous step is what stops error accumulating across a long run, so the bound is absolute however many points come out. Endpoints and `sharp` vertices are never touched. For each pin (another road's endpoint) only the **NEAREST** vertex is protected — the distinction matters because the debris clusters precisely AROUND a junction, so a radius generous enough to recognise a junction drawn with slack would otherwise protect the very clutter this removes. Pin radius scales with the road's own width (`w/8`, floor 0.75 ft, cap 4 ft), matching B1011's junction-coincidence rule. Wired inside `migrateRoad` so **both read paths** get it (the B1012 lesson).
- **Result on his plan: 36 control points → 20.** All three tees, all three drives and the dissolved region survive unchanged. The new pass moves the road at most **0.90 ft** on his plan (inside its 1.5 ft budget); the only larger correction, 2.04 ft on the truck loop, is B1010's pre-existing debris pass doing what it was shipped to do.
