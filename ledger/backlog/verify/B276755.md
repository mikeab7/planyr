- **MEASURED, on the owner's own parcels, on the same service seconds apart:** Will Clayton's county query built to **2325 characters → HTML 404**; Bain's, at **1512 → 200**. `services.arcgis.com` (IIS) rejects a /query whose URL runs past roughly 2 KB of query string, and **a 404 decodes as "this layer has nothing here."**
- **So the county and the ETJ came back EMPTY on any site whose boundary was finely digitised — deterministically, as a function of how many vertices the surveyor drew, while looking exactly like "no ETJ here."** This is what B209507 recorded as the ETJ source being *"measurably flaky — 0 at three of six points"*: it was never flakiness. Before the fix **8 of 27 sites** could not resolve their county or ETJ; after it, **none**.
- **Root cause:** `simplifyRing` bounded the VERTEX COUNT (80), which is the wrong quantity — 80 lon/lat pairs at full double precision is ~2.5 KB of query string.
- **Fixed, two bounds:** coordinates are rounded to **6 decimal places** (~4 inches, far finer than any boundary layer's own precision, and most of the saving on its own), and the vertex count then walks a ladder down until the built URL fits `MAX_QUERY_URL`, never below a triangle. A coarser intersect test beats no test at all. POST would remove the ceiling outright but the B445 same-origin cache proxy is GET-addressed, so a POST body would silently bypass the cache — bounded GET keeps both.
