- **⛔ THE DEFECT, and it was aimed at the highest-value signal in the programme.** `clientErrors.sink` handed the insert promise two empty handlers, under a comment stating the consequence: *"swallows all errors (including a missing-table / RLS rejection) so a telemetry failure is itself invisible."* Downstream, the owner's **"that felt slow just now"** button showed **✓ "Recorded — thanks"** off the LOCAL capture succeeding. So the one signal that comes from the person who actually has the symptom was also the one best able to vanish without trace — and B1121's whole disposition (*"instrument it so it captures itself"*) would have failed quietly for a week before anyone asked why no data had arrived. That is precisely the rot the NEVER-PARK stopping rule exists to prevent, so **the stopping rule was only sound once the pipe was proven.**
- **THE PROOF, link by link — full record in `docs/perf/CAPTURE-PIPE.md`.** Deployed build `c6a4b94` carries the recorder chunk and arms it · an induced stall produces captures against the real trigger · each becomes a real POST to `/rest/v1/client_errors` · every row 1,687–1,765 of the 2,000-char cap, parsed back and its packed frame track decoded (64–128 frames) · **the RLS policy was executed for real against the production table as BOTH `anon` and `authenticated`** (with a live JWT claim), the row landing attributed to the caller's `user_id`, then selected back and parsed in SQL · **the `manual` kind round-trips**, warm (128 frames) and cold.
- **⛔ THE ONE HOP THIS SANDBOX CANNOT MAKE, stated rather than glossed.** The egress gateway answers **403 to CONNECT** for `*.supabase.co`, so no browser here can complete the real request. It is bridged, not skipped: the harness INTERCEPTS at the network layer (so what is asserted is the production bundle's actual POST, not a mock's) and the exact intercepted row is then inserted server-side under the real roles. What remains is his machine, his session, his network — V62544.
- **⛔ AND A TRAP WORTH KEEPING.** `INSERT … RETURNING` FAILS under this policy — `RETURNING` needs a SELECT policy and there deliberately is none — and it fails with wording that reads exactly like a rejected insert. The client must never `.select()` on this insert, and it does not. Consequence: a browser can only ever prove *the server accepted the write*. **A SELECT policy was considered and deliberately NOT added** — the write-only posture is a stated decision in `CLAUDE.md`, and changing a security posture to make a test easier is the wrong trade.
- **WHAT SHIPPED.** `sink` returns its outcome and keeps `lastSend` / `delivery` on `window.pfTelemetry` (readable live, no database round trip), with **one** bounded retry — one, not a queue: a telemetry channel that retries forever becomes the load. It still never throws into the app and never reports its own failure through itself. The recorder tracks delivery per capture and counts the undelivered. **The button's ✓ now means DELIVERED**: *sending* → ✓ on acknowledgement, or a warning saying the capture is kept on the device and could not reach the server.
- **THE ANTI-ROT ARM.** `ui-audit/verify-capture-pipe.mjs` (`npm run perf:capturepipe`) runs five arms; `rejected` answers the same insert with 401 and requires the button and `lastSend` to say so. **Before this item that arm was un-failable** — nothing in the app could observe a rejection, so no assertion about one could ever have gone red.
