- **FEMA travels; everything around it did not.** The `flood` source is the national NFHL and zone classification is zone-letter logic, so Colorado sites get real flood zones today. But `floodAdministrator.js`'s `RULE_KEY_ALIAS` is entirely Texas, and `DEFAULT_FLOODPLAIN_RULES` has six Texas jurisdictions and no Colorado record — so compensating storage is **unwired in Colorado** and now reports itself as such through B1104's capability matrix rather than going quiet.
- **CWCB checked rather than assumed, and it cuts the OTHER way.** Colorado has a **statewide floor stricter than FEMA** in three specific ways (2 CCR 408-1, recorded in `lib/coloradoRegions.js` `CO_STATE_FLOOD_STANDARD`): **1 ft freeboard** above the 100-yr for new/substantially-improved structures (NFIP requires only at-or-above BFE); **2 ft for Critical Facilities** (NFIP has no separate standard); **0.5 ft floodway designation rise** for newly studied/revised reaches (FEMA's default is 1.0 ft). Unlike Texas, where freeboard is purely a local-ordinance matter, a Colorado site therefore has a known minimum before any local ordinance is read. It is a **floor** — locals commonly adopt more, and a hazardous-materials building can be a Critical Facility even on an industrial site. Marked `verified: true, secondarySource: true`: the CWCB's own adopted-rules PDF returned HTTP 403 to this environment, so the values are triangulated from the rule text as published through the Colorado SoS CCR and Cornell LII — confirm subsection lettering against the primary PDF.
- **C.R.S. 37-92-602(8) wired as a rule record** (`lib/drawdownStatute.js`) — ≥97% of a 5-year storm released within **72 hr**, ≥99% of larger events within **120 hr**, plus the post-5-Aug-2015 **State Engineer notification** (location, surface area at design volume, drain-rate data) and the rebuttable presumption of no material injury. Cheap because the engine already existed: `drawdownTime.js`'s time-to-empty feeds it directly.
- **Two design points worth carrying forward.** (1) **It never reports "pass."** The screening drawdown is a constant-rate *lower* bound — real outflow decays as head drops — so a result inside the limit means non-compliance is **not ruled out**, never that the facility complies; a failure, by the same logic, is real. Verdicts are `fail` / `not-ruled-out` / `unknown`. (2) **It fails the facility, not the average**: the statute applies per facility, so one slow pond fails the site even when the site-wide figure is inside the limit.
- **Texas presentation is unchanged** — `assessStatutoryDrawdown` returns `applies:false` for any state but CO, and the existing informational readout renders exactly as before. Proven by B1107's golden master.
