- **The brief's premise was half wrong, and the important half.** It assumed Colorado has "no equivalent middle or bottom tier" to Texas's chain. There **IS** a statewide bottom tier: the Colorado OIT **Colorado Public Parcels** composite, county-scopable by `countyName` — the exact TxGIO analogue. So a Colorado county outage degrades to the same honest statewide backup a Texas one does, not to nothing. (No clean *middle* tier exists — DRCOG is metro-only, PPACG El Paso-only — so none is modelled.) Also corrected: the real fallback chain is **two** tiers (county CAD → statewide), not the three the brief described; there is no city tier and no regional tier in the parcel path.
- **Endpoints VERIFIED LIVE 2026-07-29** (queried, metadata read, feature count returned): **Adams** 188,723 · **Denver** 240,360 (layer id **245**, not 0) · **Weld** 163,685 · **Broomfield** 27,531 — all ArcGIS-Online hosted, CORS-open, no key. Plus `GIS_SOURCES.countyCo` (Colorado statewide county boundaries, 64 polygons) and, as a Texas control re-probe, Harris 1,548,457 and Fort Bend 385,648.
- **Endpoints NOT shipped because they could not be probed:** Arapahoe, Larimer, Boulder, El Paso ride the statewide composite with their county-own endpoint parked in `candidateUrl` **with provenance** (a registered ArcGIS Online item — owner, item id — and the reason it was not probed). **Jefferson** has no locatable county parcel endpoint at all and rides the composite outright, exactly as Waller does in Texas. → `V513`.
- **Key collision handled:** Texas and Colorado both have an **El Paso** and a **Jefferson** county. Texas keys are persisted in saved plans and could not be renamed (Constraint 1), so Colorado keys are `co_`-prefixed and `countyKeyForName(name)` stays **Texas-only** unless a state is passed — an unqualified `"El Paso"` returns null rather than guessing.
- **The Texas regression the harness caught, and it was the dangerous one:** `candidateCountiesForPoint`'s out-of-bbox branch returned every configured county, `harris` first — and the Layers-panel jurisdiction resolver reads `candidate[0]`. A Colorado click would have inherited **Harris County**, which is exactly the wrong-but-plausible answer this work exists to prevent. The fallback is now state-scoped; a Texas point's candidate list is byte-identical. B1107's golden master caught this AND a second regression (Colorado's composite leaking into Texas candidates) before either shipped.
- **County identify is now region-routed like ETJ already was** (`countySourcesForPoint`): a Colorado point resolves against Colorado's boundary layer; every Texas point and every point outside Colorado gets the **identical** `JURISDICTION_SOURCES.county` object — asserted by identity, not equality.
- **`ui-audit/gis-source-audit.mjs` extended (check #3, county parcel provenance).** `counties.js` is exempt from the no-inline-URL rule, and that exemption is what would have let a guessed URL ship. Every county row must now declare its `state` and be live-verified, on its state's composite, or carry a `verifiedNote` saying why not; a `candidateUrl` requires `candidateProvenance` and may never be the primary. Wired to CI via `test/gisSources.test.js`.
