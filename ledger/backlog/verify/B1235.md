Read straight off layer 28's `drawingInfo.renderer`, FEMA paints FIVE more `FLD_ZONE = X` subtypes in its **"0.2% Annual Chance Flood Hazard"** class: `1 PCT DEPTH LESS THAN 1 FOOT` (**30,981** polygons nationally), `1 PCT DRAINAGE AREA LESS THAN 1 SQUARE MILE` (**23,157**), and the `PERCENT`-spelled twins of every `PCT` form. Those are shaded Zone X on the FIRM — so **COH Ch.19's FFE-at-the-0.2%-WSE rule, Fort Bend's Interim Atlas-14 §9 offset and Waller Art. 5 §A(8) all reach them** — and the app was classifying every one as "no flood hazard": no trigger, no mitigation volume, and minimal-risk grey on the map. Counts measured against the live service.
- **The fix is ONE reader, not three regexes.** New pure `lib/floodZone.js` classifies a polygon into eight named variants from FEMA's own renderer, and the mitigation ledger, the screening analysis and the map paint all read it. Two orderings it settles that the old regexes got wrong on real data: a `1 PCT FUTURE CONDITIONS, FLOODWAY` polygon is **not** the regulatory floodway (68,920 nationally — it is FEMA's future-conditions class), and a levee `AREA WITH REDUCED FLOOD RISK` X is **not** the 0.2% band (24,830). Both are now named states rather than being silently folded into "no hazard".
