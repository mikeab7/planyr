**The report.** After B1184 the panel offered *By side* / *Per segment* and listed "Side 1 · 2 seg, Side 2 · 6 seg, Side 3 · 10 seg … Side 15". Fifteen rows where there had been sixty is real progress, and the segment counts are a good touch — but it is not what was asked for. The owner does not think in fifteen geometric sides; he thinks in the four setbacks an ordinance writes: **FRONT, SIDE, STREET SIDE (corner), REAR**. Exactly one row had resolved to "Front", so role detection existed and fired once — it was never carried through to the other fourteen.

**What shipped.**
- **A THIRD tier, `By role`, and it is now the DEFAULT** (`sbEditMode` starts at `"role"`). Four rows — Front / Side / Street side / Rear — each one input writing to every side in that role through the same canonical per-edge `pc.setbacks` array. *By side* stays the middle tier and *Per segment* the exception tier, both behaving exactly as before. A role no side currently carries still gets its row (so the four are always the four) but shows an em-dash instead of an input — there is nothing to write to yet. The mode toggle is no longer gated on the boundary being multi-segment: `By role` changes what the list means on a four-sided lot too.
- **EVERY side is auto-assigned on load, not one.** New pure module `lib/setbackRoles.js`. The order is deliberate, each step narrowing what the next can claim: (1) **street abutment** — a run most of whose length lies within 150 ft of a road centerline *and whose outward normal faces it* (distance alone marks a lot's deep side street-abutting merely because its far end reaches the corner); (2) **Front** — the longest abutting run, or simply the longest run when the plan carries no roads, plus any run facing the same way at the same depth, so a frontage broken by a jog is one Front; (3) **Street side** — any OTHER abutting run, the corner-lot case, and only ever from real road geometry — never guessed; (4) **Rear** — runs facing roughly opposite the front; (5) **Side** — the rest. Roles reason over runs built from a FLAT value vector, so typing a setback cannot reshuffle the labels.
- **The assignment is visible and correctable.** Each *By side* row carries a role picker showing the resolved role; changing it writes the role to every edge in that side (stored per edge in `pc.roles`, aligned to the ring exactly like `pc.setbacks`, and re-derived rather than shifted by one if the boundary is reshaped). The user's assignment always wins over the auto one.
- **Map chips read their role** — "Front · 25′", not a bare 25′. The plate now sizes itself to its text, so two consequences had to be handled: `spaceOut` gained a **box** metric beside its radial one (same helper, not a fork — a role chip is three times as wide as a number plate and exactly as tall, so one circular threshold either lets two wide chips overlap side-by-side or throws away vertically-stacked ones with plenty of air between them), and the inboard step now clears the plate's own half-extent instead of a fixed offset, so a wide chip can no longer straddle the boundary and land on the OUTBOARD side-length dimension B215 deliberately put on the other side of the edge.

**NON-NEGOTIABLE — no site's computed buildable area may change. Proven against a real production snapshot.** A role is a LABEL over edges and is never an input to a measurement; nothing in the role layer writes a setback VALUE, so the invariant holds by construction. To make that *provable* rather than merely argued, `offsetPolygon` (and `lineIntersect`) were lifted verbatim out of `SitePlanner.jsx` into `lib/parcelOffset.js` — the one geometric statement this app cannot get wrong had lived as a private function in a 20,000-line component and was only ever checkable by rendering. `test/fixtures/weldParcelProduction.json` is the owner's Weld County parcel read straight out of `planyr_production` (`sites.id sms7v3ua7ksy`, 60 vertices, geometry only — no owner name or address rides into the repo), and the tests assert on it that the per-edge setbacks stay byte-identical and that the ring `offsetPolygon` produces is `toEqual`-identical across role assignment AND reassignment. The browser harness asserts the same thing on the live DOM, and separately that the ring DOES move once a number is typed.
