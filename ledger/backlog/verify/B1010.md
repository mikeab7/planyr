`[ ]` **⏳ merged — V456 PENDING (Verify: live — `Blocker: real-data`).** DISTANCE was the wrong test.
- **Root cause, measured on the live geometry:** the connect that made the new tee dropped a vertex **3.4 ft** from one already on the truck loop — just outside B1008's flat 1.5 ft collapse. The alignment swings **37°** across that stub. A 40 ft road cannot bend 37° in 3.4 ft, so the arc fillet clamped to a **5 ft** radius against the truck class's 50 ft minimum (which is exactly what B1009's new flag was reporting), and — because `roadRunFrom` read the junction tangent at a fixed 1.5 ft — the tee stood ON the stub and built the entrance square to a heading the road abandons three feet later. Hence the spike. The SAME road carries harmless ~2 ft stubs further east: harmless because it runs dead straight through them. **What makes a stub dangerous is a stub carrying a TURN.**
- **Fixed (three places):** (1) `dedupeRoadVertices` gains a geometric test — a segment shorter than a quarter of the road's own width across which the alignment deflects more than ~5° collapses too, and a `pinned` list (every other road's endpoints, supplied by `migrateRoads`) guarantees cleanup can never drop a vertex a tee is welded to; (2) `roadRunFrom` reads the tangent over `roadTangentNoise(el)` = half the travel width instead of a fixed 1.5 ft, so a few feet of debris cannot aim a junction; (3) `planRoadConnect` reuses an existing control point within the same width-scaled tolerance, so the debris stops being created.
