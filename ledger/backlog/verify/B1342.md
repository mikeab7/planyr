- **The owner's words, and the correction to my first reading.** He first reported it as *"some row names decide not to appear above the bar"*, and I began hunting a silent-drop. He then corrected it: *"the task name is IN THE MIDDLE of the Gantt chart bar — which we have talked about before and you proposed — but I don't want it in the middle. I just want it ABOVE the bar just like every other bar."* The labels were never missing. On some rows they were painted **on** the bar, and the defect was the inconsistency.
- **WHERE IT CAME FROM, cited as the owner asked. `B393` (2026-06-22).** Its stated goal was *"never painted on the bar FILL"* — the reported bug then was centred names being covered by the bar. Its answer for **Center** alignment was not to move the name off the bar but to back it with a white **contrast plate** whenever the name fitted inside the bar; **Left**/**Right** placed it inline before/after the bar. So the on-bar label shipped deliberately, and it worked as designed. **The problem it was protecting against — a name wider than its bar — is now handled by the same above-the-bar caption every other row already used**, which is why reverting it recreates nothing.
- **REPRODUCED FIRST, on the owner's REAL data, not a fixture.** `e2e/fixtures/schedules/grand-port.fixture.json` is his actual 213-task Grand Port program, pulled from `planyr_production` (`planar_data` `hs-v1` → `projects['2']`), `labelAlign:"center"` included — that setting is the condition, so the fixture must keep it. Driving the real Schedule app in Split view at 33 % zoom, **exactly 8 of the 168 visible rows** rendered `mode="plate"` (name centred on the bar): *Complete Draft TIA · TIA Review #1 · AHJ Review #2 · AHJ Review #3 · CP Review · Underground Terms & Conditions · Develop Drawings ×2*. **What they have in common:** they are leaf tasks (no children) whose bar at that zoom is WIDER than their own name — literally `labelW <= bw - 4`, the B393 Center branch. Nothing to do with clipping, collision, the viewport edge or the top row. The first sampling of the harness saw **zero** defects because the chart is vertically virtualised and the offending rows are ~90 rows down; it now sweeps the whole pane, which is the only reason this was measurable at all.
- **THE FIX — one rule, and `align` demoted to what it should always have been.** `placeGanttLabel` no longer branches on `kind`: **every** name, for every kind and every alignment, is a caption in the clear air ABOVE its glyph. `align` now chooses only the HORIZONTAL anchor — Left → the bar's start, Center → its midpoint, Right → its end — so the Left/Center/Right control still means something and all three sit at the same height. `plate`, `before` and `after` are gone from both render paths, along with the contrast backing that existed only to make an on-bar label legible.
- **`fitCaptionFs` generalised.** B629 capped a SUMMARY caption to the headroom above its bracket; now that every name is a caption, leaves and milestones read the same cap. A no-op at the default row height, and what keeps the rule honest at compressed ones.
- **PDF-PARITY — the export needed real work, not a mirror.** The helper is shared, so the print names went above too — onto glyphs the exhibit drew VERTICALLY CENTRED in an 18 px row, leaving a caption nowhere to go. Print now bottom-aligns EVERY glyph on one baseline (`GLYPH_BASE`), which is what the on-screen chart has always done and closes a screen↔print drift that predates this item: new `leafBand` / `mileBand` / `summaryBand` helpers are read by the bar renderer, the name placement **and** the dependency router, so an arrow can never attach to where a glyph used to be. The print milestone diamond goes from a fixed ±5 to ±4 so its caption clears it on an 18 px row.
- **THE B1188 INVARIANT STILL HOLDS, and is now trivially true.** No branch returns nothing: a caption too wide for the chart is SLID horizontally by `clampX` until it fits. Collision-driven declutter does not exist on this surface and is not being introduced — rows are disjoint vertical bands carrying one name each, so a name can only contend with itself. The distinction the owner asked me not to collapse is therefore preserved by construction: there is no fit failure that can hide a label, and no declutter rule to confuse it with.
