- **Report.** The "Parcel 62.7 ac" badge rendered *"well to the right of the parcel, floating over neighbouring land rather than over the parcel it labels."*
- **Root cause, confirmed in the code rather than guessed.** The anchor was the ring's plain **vertex average** — not a centre of anything. It is pulled toward whichever side was digitized with the most points, and on this parcel the subdivision edge carries dozens of short segments while the opposite side is two long ones, so the average slid hard toward the dense edge and off the lot. (The owner's own diagnosis named the area centroid; on a long bent strip that one can land outside the polygon too, so neither was safe.)
- **The fix.** New pure `lib/polylabel.js` — the **pole of inaccessibility**, the centre of the largest circle that fits inside the ring, found by the classic best-first quadtree search. It is always inside, it sits in the visually fattest part of the shape, and it leaves the most clear room around the plate. Written out (~40 lines of arithmetic) rather than added as a runtime dependency, and memoised per points array so a hundreds-of-vertex parcel pays for the search once, not once per frame. The user's dragged `labelOffset` still applies on top; `centroid` remains the fallback for a degenerate ring.
- **Verified here.** Unit tests: the dense-on-one-side strip (the exact report — vertex average lands on the dense edge, the pole lands in the body of the strip), a deep U whose AREA centroid is provably outside while the pole is inside, an L, a flag lot and a triangle, memoisation, and degenerate input answered rather than thrown on.
