It was the most likely real defect of the three, and it was the only one whose *behaviour* had genuinely changed.
- **WHAT CHANGED, EXACTLY.** Until 2026-08-07 a double-tap on a building's dimension number at its DEFAULT position opened the inline length editor. **B50010 (commit `acd99a0d`, PR #932, 2026-08-07)** reversed that in `onDimNumberDown` — *"WHEN THE NUMBER SITS ON THE BODY, THE BODY WINS"*. The report behind it was a centreline road whose width number is anchored to the centreline midpoint and therefore painted ON the pavement, so a double-click aimed at the road could not miss it and the width chip swallowed a gesture the click contract says opens Properties. B50010 fixed it *"once in the shared dispatch for every type, not special-cased for road"* — and a building is caught by the same rule because **B592 clamps its number ONTO the footprint**. The case went red in BOTH lanes on 2026-08-08, the day after that merged.
- **WHY THE SPEC WAS THE STALE SIDE, not the code.** `CLAUDE.md`'s **CHROME-NEVER-EATS-A-PRESS** now cites B50010 as the standing rule (*"chrome sitting over its own object's BODY forwards the press to the body, asked once for every type"*), and `dblclick-properties.spec.js:210` already asserts the same contract for a road. Two specs were asserting opposite things; the contract is settled and this one had not been updated.
- **⛔ RETARGETED, NOT REWRITTEN — the coverage GREW.** Deleting the assertion, or reducing it to "Properties opens", would have quietly killed B912's feature and duplicated `chrome-swallows-press`. B50010 kept the inline editor *"exactly where the number is genuinely the only thing there"*, and that path had no guard anywhere. So the case is now TWO tests pinning both halves of B50010's rule: **(a)** number at its default position, on the footprint → Properties opens and the numeric editor does **not**; **(b)** number slid clear of the footprint → the editor opens and the building resizes to the typed depth, exactly as B912 always asserted.
- **THE MECHANISM THAT MAKES (b) REACHABLE, measured rather than assumed.** The number renders OUTBOARD of the dimension line (6 px beyond it, `textAnchor="end"`) and `dimSlideRange` lets the line slide to the end of the footprint — so sliding the dimension along parks the NUMBER in clear space. Measured on this build: it moves from x≈264 to x≈175 against a footprint whose left edge is x≈204, i.e. fully clear, `dimOffset.x = −252`, and the editor opens. Test (b) asserts that clearance explicitly, so if a future change re-clamps the number onto the body it fails **there**, loudly, instead of silently becoming a duplicate of (a).
