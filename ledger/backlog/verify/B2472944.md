### B2472944 — Site header showed a city AND another city's ETJ at once ("City of Sugar Land · Houston ETJ · Fort Bend County" on Concept A): city limits now outrank any ETJ, in the one identify every surface reads `[Site Planner / jurisdiction header]` (bug) #site-planner #gis #floodplain #testing  *(owner chat block 2026-10-28 — "it says Houston ETJ but then also says City of Sugar Land, and it can't be both. So figure that out." Concept A, sites.id smvsphq82qqu, Fort Bend. Minted via `npm run next-id -- --against-main`; branch `claude/gracious-ride-igaj6u`.)*
**Ground truth (measured live 2026-10-28, not assumed):** Concept A's saved parcel is ONE tract of **316.6 acres, 99.998% inside City of Sugar Land's full-purpose limits** (it is not split, and it is not in Houston's ETJ). Four independent sources agree: Sugar Land's limits are the same in TxGIO and Fort Bend CAD (`City_View`); Houston's ETJ polygon is the same in H-GAC and in Houston's OWN planning layer (`COH_ETJ_view` / `gisweb02 …/Administrative_Boundary/MapServer/1`). The two layers overlap on a band north of the airport (~8% of a 5×4 km window), and Houston's polygon grazes **0.73% of the tract (~2.3 ac)** — just over the 0.5% "counts as touching" floor, so the ETJ-share pass reported it and the label printed shape `in-city-etj`. Hypothesis (b) (ETJ not clipped by annexation) is the cause, but it is the publishers' own data, not an H-GAC staleness quirk — Houston's layer says the same. The origin point alone (29.6122132, -95.6693177) is inside the limits and in no ETJ polygon.
**Cause in code:** `formatJurisdictionBadge` deduped an ETJ only against the SAME city whose limits hold the site (`samePlace`), so a DIFFERENT city's ETJ over ground inside the limits survived; `jurisdictionLabel` then had a deliberate "in-city-etj" shape for it. An ETJ is by definition the ground outside every city's limits (Local Gov't Code ch. 42), so that shape described two layers overlapping, not a place.
**Fix (one source of truth — `identifyJurisdiction`, which the header badge, Site Analysis rows, the detention reviewer and the floodplain administrator all read):** (1) the ETJ share pass is measured NET of every city's full-purpose limits (`jurisdictionShare.subtractNorm`, `jurisdiction.fetchCityClipOut`, `identifyCityShares` `clipOutFn`, cache key `:net1`); the removed share is kept as `etjCityOverlap` so the overlap is DISCLOSED (badge tooltip `etjOverlapNote`, Who-governs note) not hidden; (2) floor for a bare point / failed netting: a city that holds the WHOLE site leaves no ETJ (`out.etj = []`); (3) at the leaf, `jurisdictionLabel` has no "in-city-etj" shape and `formatJurisdictionBadge` returns `etjLabels: []` for a site a city holds, so the floodplain administrator no longer raises a second city's ETJ rule on it. A genuinely SPLIT site is untouched (its other part is where an ETJ legitimately lives). Generic, not a Sugar Land case: a regional survey (H-GAC ETJ × TxGIO limits, Houston metro) found **36 cross-city pairs** where one city's limits sit under another city's published ETJ polygon (Humble ← Houston 6,429 ac; Sugar Land ← Houston 769 ac; Katy, Pasadena, La Porte, Waller, Prairie View, Tomball, Missouri City, Pearland … ← Houston; League City, Seabrook, Kemah, Galveston, Dickinson, Friendswood … ← Texas City; Plum Grove ← Splendora; Rosenberg ← Pleak; …) — `ui-audit/audit-etj-city-overlap.mjs` re-runs it.
