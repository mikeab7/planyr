`[x]` **MECHANISM, measured on the deployed build rather than reasoned from the source.** The left grip opens its blank margin INSIDE the sheet (`sheetGrowLeft`), so the body genuinely moves right inside the scroller's own content, and the ONLY thing holding the words still on screen is the compensating scroll in `NoteEditor.jsx`'s `sheetGrowLeft`-keyed layout effect. **A scroll is a BOUNDED resource.** `scrollLeft` can only move as far as `scrollWidth − clientWidth` allows, and the mat's gutter (`matPadX`) is deliberately pin-INDEPENDENT — sized against the natural card, never against the page's own width — so a page NARROWER than that card leaves the mat's content short of the pane, with no horizontal overflow at all. The write is silently clamped, the compensation under-delivers, and the words slide right by exactly what could not be spent. Nothing notices, because that effect compares CONTENT-space positions and never the achieved SCREEN position, so the loss is permanent and is never corrected on a later frame. It is also discrete — one bite per `pointermove` — which is precisely what *"in little intervals"* describes. Instrumented directly, the setter recorded `{ before: 0, want: 140, got: 0, max: 0 }`: the browser refused the entire compensation in one go.
