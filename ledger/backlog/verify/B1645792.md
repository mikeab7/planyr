`[x]` **FIXED AND RED-PROOFED.** `driveJunctionsOf` (SitePlanner.jsx) — the road→parking-drive/truck-court/paving-pad junction render — calls `roadGeometry.teeGeometry` with the target rect's facing edge as the "through" line (`phT: 0`, since a pad is a separate element with its own fill, never unioned with the road's pavement). **Grepped: `teeGeometry` has exactly one live caller** — road-to-road tees moved to `nodeJunction` after B1011 and never touch this function, so the fix below cannot affect road-to-road geometry.

**ROOT CAUSE, measured.** The old wedge builder anchored the SIDE (driveway) reach to `f.tan2` — a point on the driveway's THEORETICAL long-edge line, extended infinitely — and pushed it perpendicular by a small fixed `deepS`. That line is the driveway's REAL physical edge only where its tessellated strip actually reaches, and the strip's own end (at the connect point) is a FLAT CAP perpendicular to the driveway's centerline (`etOpenButt`), not to that line. At an oblique approach the two corners are NOT symmetric about that cap: on a 36 ft drive meeting a pad edge at 45°, one corner's `tan2` lands ~9 ft further INTO the strip than the flat cap (harmless extra overlap) while the OTHER lands ~9 ft BEHIND it, on the side of the cap the strip's own polygon never occupies. A fixed perpendicular push from `tan2` runs parallel to the cap it needs to cross, so past roughly a 40° approach angle the wedge on that side dissolves as its OWN disconnected pavement island — the strip's real, now-exposed flat cap is what's left, forming the raw diagonal edge the owner saw, with open ground (a real gap in pavement coverage) between it and the stranded return: the knife-edge notch on one side, a hard unfilleted corner on the other. Road-to-road tees never show this — `nodeJunction` gives the "through" side real pavement width, so a side road's own flat cap lands somewhere inside that wide strip by sheer overlap, a coincidence of a wide target that a zero-width pad doesn't have.

**THE FIX** (`roadGeometry.js`, `teeGeometry`'s wedge builder): anchor the wedge to the driveway's REAL flat-cap corner (`T + inS*phS` — the same point `roadStripRing`'s own flat cap uses), reaching a small tuck past it into the strip, then take the CONVEX HULL of every candidate point (the arc, the old backing points, and the new cap-anchored ones). The fillet arc is convex outward by construction, so the hull leaves the true curve untouched and only fills in whatever straight-line backing is needed to bridge it to wherever the real cap corner turns out to be — ahead of `tan2` or behind it, no case split needed.

`[x]` **THE REAL ROOT CAUSE, finally isolated with a working acceptance test (built FIRST, per the dispatch's own instruction — see B1681521/NEW-4 below).** The ×2 fix's own "add T to the hull" step is what caused this: a CONVEX HULL keeps a point only when it is geometrically EXTREME, and `capCorner` (the driveway's own real flat-cap corner — the one vertex anchoring the wedge to where the strip actually ends) is COLLINEAR with, and therefore silently DROPPED in favour of, `T` and the pad-edge tangent the instant the approach nears perpendicular (all three then sit on the pad-edge line together) — which is exactly the ×2 amendment's own change, since it is what put `T` in the hull's input set in the first place. Once `capCorner` is dropped, the hull's boundary cuts a straight diagonal from `T` down to wherever the arc/backing points land, opening the enclosed gap — measured ~124 sq ft on a plain PERPENDICULAR 36 ft drive into a 200×150 ft pad (NEW-2's own regression), and 95–126 sq ft across the whole 0–70° oblique sweep (NEW-1, this item).
