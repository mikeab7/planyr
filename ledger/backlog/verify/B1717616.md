PR #1755 (merge 429b9b2) REGRESSED three of four acute-side cases from a partial ~20° arc at R≈15 to no return at all: turn covered 0.008–0.041° of a required 116–125°, fitted radius 42–43 ft at RMS 0.14–0.17 ft — not an arc. `[x]` **PR #1755 REVERTED FIRST, as directed.** `src/workspaces/site-planner/lib/roadGeometry.js` is restored byte-for-byte to `429b9b2^1` (verified by direct diff). #1755's premise — "the same angle at opposite leans needs a different ring traversal order, so try both and take whichever is simple" — did not hold, and choosing the pavement's SHAPE by a numerical property of a vertex list is what every round of this item has done.
`[x]` **ROOT CAUSE, and it is not in `roadGeometry.js` at all — the wedge builder was never wrong.** A curb return's arc runs TANGENT into the target's edge, so the road network's dissolved ring — which holds the strips and wedges ALONE, because the pad is a separate element painted OVER it (`Z_LAYER` 1 against the road network's 0, `planStyle.js`) — carries a genuine ~177° CUSP at that tangent point. Its incoming leg is the arc's own last tessellation chord, ~1.5 ft. `collapseRingSpikes` (`roadNetwork.js`) convicted that vertex as a spurious spike; removing it exposed the NEXT arc vertex in the same cusp, so the pass CASCADED and ate the return one chord at a time. MEASURED: a complete 21-point arc (fitted R 15.155 ft, RMS 0.0002 ft) entered the union intact and left it as 5 points plus a straight 18 ft chord across the corner.
`[x]` **WHY SIX ROUNDS AND EIGHT PRs SHIPPED GREEN.** Every prior suite asserted on `teeGeometry`'s raw fillet arc (`geom.returns`), which is — and always was — complete at every angle and radius. No test ever compared the FINISHED boundary against it. #1755's own new fold test then carried an explicit exemption for turns within 1° of a right angle, so the exactly-89.95° corner it left where the arc had been walked straight through it.
`[x]` **THE FIX (`roadNetwork.js`), strictly SUBTRACTIVE — nothing that survived the old pass can now be removed.** `ringSpikes` gains a third necessary condition: a vertex is spurious only if its DRIFT (its distance from the straight segment joining its two neighbours — exactly how far the ring moves if it goes) is under `RING_SPIKE_DRIFT_FT` = 0.25 ft. The artifacts this pass was built for (B1611840 / B1690816) measured 0.04–0.05 ft; a real corner's drift is its own leg length, 1.1 ft and up. `collapseRingSpikes` additionally bounds the WHOLE pass against the ORIGINAL ring (`RING_SPIKE_PASS_DRIFT_FT` = 1 ft), so no chain of individually-redundant removals can add up to an edit — the discipline `simplifyRoadVertices` already uses, for the same reason.
