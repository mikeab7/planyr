- **AUDIT-FIRST verdict: NOT wired — and it could not have been.** `cachedVectorLayer` binds its hover/click identify only when `interactive` is true, which comes from `opts.identifyOk`. `MapFinder.jsx` passes it (`identifyOk: () => !selectModeRef.current`); `SitePlanner.jsx` never did, so no handler was bound at all. On top of that the planner's Leaflet backdrop sits inside a `pointerEvents:"none"` box — its SVG canvas owns every pointer event — so even a bound handler could never receive the click. Only the coordinate/elevation chip could answer, which is exactly what the owner saw. Making that layer vector bought nothing on the surface where a site is actually worked.
- **Fix.** The pure half moves into `vectorLayers.js` (`hitFeature` — polygon containment, line distance within a caller-supplied tolerance; `identifyRows` — the registry rows as data). `vectorOverlay.js` keeps the last painted FeatureCollection and exposes `group.identifyAt({lat,lng,tolDeg})` on BOTH cached layers, built from the same rows the Leaflet popover renders, so the two surfaces cannot drift. `layers.js` adds `identifyOverlaysAt(refs, overlays, at)`. `SitePlanner.jsx` calls it from the existing tap-on-empty-canvas path (the B735 `tapEmpty` branch) and shows a themed card at the tap: title, the registry rows, the source's caveat, the agency. Dismissed by the next press, by Esc, and by a zoom (it is anchored in screen space).

A layer in its raster tier or on its live fallback has no geometry in hand and declines rather than guessing.

- **It WAS in the hit-test set the whole time.** `bkdd_easements` carries `canvasIdentify: true`; `identifyOverlaysAt` gates on that flag plus the layer being ON; `cachedVectorLayer` exposes `identifyAt` and holds the painted FeatureCollection; the source's `minVectorZoom` is 13, well below any working site zoom, so the layer is in its vector tier (not the raster fallback, which honestly declines). No wiring was missing.
- **THE DEFECT — polygons had ZERO click tolerance.** `hitFeature` accepted a `tolDeg` and then applied it to LINES ONLY: polygons hit-tested by containment alone. So the channel centreline carried the planner's full on-screen slop on both sides while the easement band — a **70 ft** ribbon, a few pixels wide at the default site zoom — had a click target exactly its own drawn width and not a pixel more. Land beside it and the identify returned nothing, the tap fell through, and the coordinate/elevation readout answered instead. That is precisely the three failed live attempts, and it is why the channel (a line) answered on the first try while the band never did.
- **Fix.** `hitFeature` now applies the SAME tolerance to polygon ring EDGES, in **two passes**: an exact containment anywhere in the collection wins first, and only then does the near-miss fallback answer. The two-pass order is load-bearing — with adjacent polygons (two counties sharing a border) a tap just inside one is within tolerance of the other's edge, and a single-pass loop would hand back whichever came first in draw order. So the new tolerance is confined to taps that previously hit **nothing at all**; no existing answer changes. Nothing about the slop itself changed (the planner's fixed on-screen tolerance, converted to feet at the live zoom), so a wide polygon behaves exactly as before.
- **Regression guards.** `test/vectorLayers.test.js`: a real-width band answers a tap just outside it, still misses one outside the slop, and adjacent polygons keep their exact answers. `e2e/canvas-gis-identify.spec.js` adds a second spec driving the REAL planner with a **70 ft** stubbed band — it reads the planner's own slop off the canvas transform rather than assuming a zoom, taps outside the band but inside the slop, and asserts the card carries `70 ft` + `WF-10.pdf`; a tap well outside the slop still opens nothing. **That spec FAILS on the pre-fix build** (verified by reverting `vectorLayers.js` and re-running), which is what makes it a guard.
- **The interaction, documented for the live pass.** Turn **District drainage easements** ON, work at a normal site zoom (not zoomed out past the raster tier), and tap **on or beside** the band on empty canvas — not on a parcel edge, a corner handle or an element, which own their own clicks. The card is dismissed by the next press, Esc, or a zoom.
