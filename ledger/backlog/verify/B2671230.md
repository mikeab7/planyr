- **The rules (the owner's grading model, unchanged).** The WHOLE graded footprint — the pad flat at the finished floor, each side's stack falling away from its wall at the pavement slope from a dock drop (a curb drop on a non-dock side), the end parking, the 5-ft shoulder, and the slopes at the panel's ratio — must daylight inside the plan's **Limits of disturbance** (the fence: the property line ± N ft, `gradingPack.setbackRings`), no cell needs more than the panel's max cut / fill, and the BUILDING itself stays inside the setback (courts and lots need only the fence). Length grows in 56-ft bays (min 5), depth snaps to realistic rungs (20 ft; cross-dock 240–700, single-load 160–400, always including the template's own), SF is reported to the nearest 1,000.
- **How it works (no new grading math beyond the search).** `lib/fitBuilding.js` (pure; worker-safe; a generator driven in ~25 ms slices by `terrainWorker.js`, cancelable) resamples the ground onto a lattice ROTATED to each candidate orientation and tests every cell with the sweep's own rule — `buildableGround.blockByBoundary` (the disc-painting daylight test, now exported and shared with `maskAt`, shoulder added) — at the cell's required elevation; "the assembly fits here" is then a run-length question per row (right-run arrays), so no per-cell search. **Orientations:** parallel to the frontage road, to the parcel's long axis, the building's current rotation, **and to the contours** (added: without it a tilted plane is answered by the parcel's outline rather than the ground — the brief's own "tilted plane: orientation follows the contours" acceptance), plus the perpendicular of the first two, each ±10° around the best two. **FFE** in 1-ft steps across the relief (+ the stacks' fall; coarse 2-ft pass, then the feet around what mattered), then half-foot tuning of the same building to the finish that moves the least dirt. **Objective:** max SF, tie-break least net dirt; the alternative is the best SF among balanced solutions (|net| < 5% of cut). The answer is then **re-priced by the REAL grading engine** on the fitted layout (`fitTemplate.fitAssemblyEls` → `buildProposedSurface`), so the card's cut / fill / net are exactly what the Grading panel reads after Use this; the search's own estimate agrees with the engine within ~2%.
