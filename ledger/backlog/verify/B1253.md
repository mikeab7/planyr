**The defect.** `MapFinder.jsx` created its Leaflet map with `const cfg = COUNTIES_MAP.harris; // default landing view (no pre-picked county)` and `setView(cfg.center, cfg.zoom)`. Harris County was therefore the opening view for **every account on earth** — a brand-new user in Denver, Phoenix or Atlanta opened over Houston, Texas, which reads as broken software.

**OWNER DECISION — derived, never a preference.** He was explicit: **no setting, no toggle.** The rule has three cases:
1. **No sites yet → the whole continental US.** The honest empty state: the user has no work to show, so show them the country and let them search or click in.
2. **Exactly one site → that site's AREA at metro scale**, never its parcel.
3. **More than one → the DENSEST CLUSTER**, so a single distant outlier cannot drag the camera.

**Why case 3 matters concretely, today.** The owner has 26 sites around Houston (Harris, Fort Bend, Waller, Chambers) and exactly ONE in Weld County, Colorado. The correct answer for him is **Houston, unchanged from what he has now**, with the Colorado site simply not pulling the camera — and if Colorado ever becomes his denser market, the landing view follows on its own, with no action from him.

**What shipped.**
- **`src/workspaces/site-planner/lib/landingView.js` — pure, no Leaflet, no DOM, no React.** Single-linkage clustering at a 50-mile threshold (a metro's working radius), the group with the most sites wins, ties broken by the most recently updated site, then by first-seen order so the answer is deterministic for a given input. **Single-linkage is the point, not an implementation accident:** Waller to Chambers is ~75 miles — further apart than the threshold — and the Harris sites between them chain the two ends into ONE market, exactly as a person reads it. Colorado has nothing within a chain's reach of Texas.
- **ZOOM — "make sure it's appropriately zoomed out."** The cluster's bounds are fitted with generous padding, the fit is FLOORED to Leaflet's integer steps (so it errs wide, never tight), and the result is clamped so the landing view can never land closer than a metro / county-scale reading. Degenerate bounds — one site, or every site within a mile of the next — fit to infinity by construction and fall straight onto that clamp, which is exactly what the clamp is for: one project never opens zoomed to its own lot.
- **It is where the map OPENS, not a leash.** Two latches: `landedRef` (we have landed on real sites) and `userMovedRef` (the user has taken the wheel). Either one ends the landing behaviour for the session, so a late-arriving cloud site list can never yank a camera the user is already driving. `userMovedRef` is deliberately keyed on real INPUT — a press, a wheel, a drag — never on Leaflet's `movestart`/`zoomstart`, which our own programmatic `setView` also fires.
- **A landing view outside Texas now resolves its jurisdiction sensibly.** The Layers-panel follower read `candidateCountiesForPoint(...)[0]`, whose out-of-bbox answer is **harris-first BY CONTRACT** (click routing depends on that order, and `test/counties.test.js` asserts it). Reading it as a jurisdiction is the same hardcoded-Houston bug one layer down — it would have claimed Harris County over Denver. New pure `countyForView(lat, lng)` in `counties.js` answers the jurisdiction question on its own terms: a bbox hit wins (nearest county CENTER among overlapping padded boxes, so downtown Denver reads Denver and not whichever neighbour was declared first), otherwise the nearest configured county **within the point's own state**. Statewide parcel pseudo-keys are never returned — they are SOURCES, not jurisdictions. Click routing is untouched, and a test asserts it stayed untouched.
