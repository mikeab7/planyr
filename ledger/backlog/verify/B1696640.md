- **ROOT CAUSE, confirmed by reading the engine, not assumed.** A summary row's dates are the ROLLUP of its children (`rollupParentDates`); `cascadeDates` deliberately skips computing a parent's OWN date from its predecessors at all (B443248 — `if (parentIds.has(t.id)) return`), because a parent's span is never anything but that rollup. So a task naming its own parent (or any ancestor) as a predecessor closes a real cycle that the engine cannot resolve by iterating harder: the child's start derives from the parent's finish, and the parent's finish is in part derived from that same child's finish. `recomputeSchedule`'s 12-pass fixed-point cap (a runaway guard, never meant as the normal exit) just stops mid-ratchet — the un-converged, still-moving dates get saved, and the NEXT load resumes from there and ratchets further still. That is exactly the owner's observed progression, and exactly what B1701360's own forensic read of the account's `planar_history` confirmed happened, hour by hour.
- **THE FIX, all five parts of the brief:**
  - **(a) Blocked at entry.** `validatePredEdit` (B1701360's `isAncestorOfId`, reconciled) rejects a task naming its own ancestor (any depth) at entry, returning the dropped id(s) in `ancestor`; this item's own `childrenOf` addition to the SAME function's cyclic DFS extends that to any other rollup-mediated cycle shape (part (b), below). The toast gives the ancestor case its own plain-English reason ("is this task's own summary row — a summary's dates come FROM its tasks, not the other way around") rather than the generic circular-dependency message. There is no separate typeahead/picker to gate (predecessors are typed text, parsed and validated on commit) — this IS "blocked at creation."
  - **(b) A real cycle guard over the dependency graph, including the rollup edge.** `validatePredEdit`'s generic `reachesId` DFS now also walks "parent depends on every child" edges, so it catches ANY cycle shape that flows through a rollup, not just a direct ancestor reference — unit-tested with a cousin/sibling case proving no false positives and a cross-subtree case proving general coverage beyond what a bare ancestor-chain walk sees.
  - **(c) On load: detect, drop, re-derive once, one dismissible notice.** New pure `stripAncestorPredecessors` (mirrors `ancestorIdsOf`) runs before every recompute on load/import/restore, dropping ONLY the offending ancestor link(s) per task (every other predecessor is untouched — a real sibling dependency survives). `recascadeWithDrift` now threads this through and reports affected rows into a new `ancestorPredNotice` banner (separate from B836's driftNotice, so the same row isn't explained twice) — see B1696641 for the banner's own row-ID/click-to-scroll shape.
