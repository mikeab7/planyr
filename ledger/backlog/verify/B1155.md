- **READ THE HISTORY FIRST, and this is what it says.** This family has been "fixed" nine times and come back nine times, and every attempt shared one testing mistake — a MOCK of two STRAIGHT roads meeting at a tee. On a straight road the chord between two control points IS the road, so a chord-built junction looks perfect and a chord-based hit test reaches everything. Both defects in this pair are invisible to that mock by construction. So nothing here was declared fixed against one.
- **The failing case, found in the owner's real data rather than guessed.** Goose Creek **"Plan 1 (copy)"** (production site `sms69x8rb2qk`) is the plan in the screenshot — identified from the geometry, not by elimination: its 36' aisle `e1454749rlpiva` runs ~900 ft east off the 100' aisle, turns **~88°** through a vertex whose stored treatment is *unset* (so it defaults to a 25 ft **arc**), and the branch `e1454750rlpiva` is welded to that same vertex heading south. The branch's other end also drive-tees into a truck court, and so does the through road's — the third topology the brief names, present in the same cluster.
- **Root cause: the junction node is not on the road.** A junction is detected at a stored control POINT, and every arm's tangent, half-width offset and curb-return corner is measured from there. But a fillet at that vertex carries the DRAWN centerline clear of it — **9.9 ft** here, on a road whose pavement is 18.5 ft from centerline to back of curb. Two consequences, both visible in the owner's screenshot: the branch's edges sit ~10 ft inboard of the through road's, so the outline STEPS through the split; and the two through arms, read off the chords, come out **179.1°** apart — over the 178° "flat" threshold — so that whole armpit is skipped as a straight run-through and gets **no curb return at all**, leaving a hard square corner beside a clean one.
- **Fix — a junction is where two centerlines MEET, so make them meet.** `roadJunctionVerticesOf` collects every vertex another road tees onto; `roadCenterline` gains `sharpAt`, which renders those corners as hard corners, so the through road passes through the node its branch is welded to. The rounding that the fillet used to provide is then supplied where it belongs — by the curb returns — which is what `nodeJunction`'s new `roundOwnCorner` is for: with the vertex flattened, the road's own buffer miters that corner, so the junction must round it too or a three-way intersection ends up with one square corner and two curb returns. Standard civil practice, and it dissolves cleanly at any angle because nothing is measured against a line the pavement doesn't follow.
- **It is a NO-OP on every straight tee.** `arcCorner` already degenerates to a pass-through on a collinear vertex, and two nearly-opposite through arms are skipped as flat either way — so an ordinary tee renders byte-identically (asserted directly, and by the untouched B1011 suite). Every road-geometry read takes the same set — pavement, curb stripes, paved-area figure and length — so they describe one road.
- **Guards that FAIL on current main, which is the whole point.** `e2e/road-split-curved.spec.js` seeds the owner's real plan and asserts through the live render path: the branch and its through road are ONE region; the outline turns no more than 30° anywhere within a lane of the node (a curb return turns a few degrees per step — a right angle is a step or a notch); BOTH armpits carry a real tessellated return; no sliver holes; the square tee and the drive junctions are unaffected; building z-order preserved. **Proven red on main**: the same spec, run against a build of `main`, fails exactly those two tests (`wedges` 1 instead of 2, worst outline turn well past a right angle) and passes the other five. `test/roadCurvedJunction.test.js` carries the same in pure form, including two tests that DOCUMENT main's numbers so the guard can never go vacuous.
