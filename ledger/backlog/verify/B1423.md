- **The cause was not one bad number, it was the absence of a shared one.** Every floating thing over a map picked its own z-index at its own call site, months apart: the planner's Layers + View column **6**, the canvas furniture (north arrow, scale bar) **7**, the zoom stack **6 but later in the DOM**, and the map finder's panels **1000** — tying with Leaflet's own control containers, which are 1000 and later in the DOM. **A tie is resolved by document order**, which is why it read as random. `lib/mapChromeStack.js` is now the one model: passive furniture → map controls → **an open panel wins**, because a panel is the thing being read and operated and there is no case where a scale bar covering a slider is the right outcome. The View popover beside it is fixed by the same change rather than separately.
- **The panel is taller and wider, from the room that actually exists.** The height was a flat `62vh` (planner) and a flat `260px` (finder) — the owner saw about four rows of a twenty-eight layer list. Both are now derived from the space between the panel's top edge and the bottom furniture, and **group headers stick** so you can still tell which group you are in after scrolling. Measured after: **17 rows visible without scrolling**.
- **`minWidth: 0` is load-bearing, not tidiness.** A flex item will not shrink below its min-content width, so the row label refused to narrow, wrapped onto a second line, and pushed the agency chip past the panel's `overflow: hidden` edge — which is exactly why "FEMA flood zones" was on two lines with its chip clipped to "FEM". Every row is one line at any panel width now, with the full text on hover.
- **⛔ THE SPEC TOOK THREE ATTEMPTS TO BE HONEST, and that is worth recording.** v1 walked a grid and asked `elementFromPoint` who was on top — it **passed with the fix deliberately reverted**, because `elementFromPoint` honours `pointer-events: none` and the scale bar is exactly that: it never intercepts a click, it just paints over the panel. A hit-test is structurally blind to this bug. v2 asserted the rectangles must not intersect at all — too strict, and wrong: the panel now spans most of the map's height, so the scale bar sits inside its rectangle by construction. Overlap is not the defect; being painted OVER is. **v3** finds the chrome that paints AND overlaps, forces `pointer-events: auto` on each so the hit-test can see it, and asks who is on top at the shared pixels — isolating PAINT ORDER from pointer policy. Reverted, it fails and names the culprits: the scale-bar SVG and the `gbtn` zoom buttons.
- The sticky-header spec had the same problem in miniature (it passed with the rule removed, because a panel tall enough not to scroll leaves the header where it was) and now **proves the list actually scrolled** before asserting stickiness. A guard that cannot distinguish "sticky" from "nothing moved" is not a guard.
