Originally done 2026-06-19 (branch `claude/sharp-edison-7gdn3y`).** Edge-case testing on irregular parcels (L-shapes, flag lots, curved frontage, dense survey boundaries, triangles) surfaced two issues, both fixed. **(1) Concave inward placement (refines B215):** the setback pill / boundary-dim fan chose the inboard side by stepping *toward the parcel centroid* — but on a concave / L-shaped / flag-lot parcel the centroid can sit in a notch (or outside the polygon), so a pill was thrown to the WRONG side (outside the lot). Replaced with a **point-in-ring test** (a small step that must land INSIDE the boundary) via a new `inwardScreenNormal` helper (computed in feet, mapped to screen), used by the per-side pill, the per-segment pill, AND the run-length dimension — correct on any shape. **(2) Gentle-curve grouping (refines B214):** `edgeRuns` now chains each edge to the **PREVIOUS** edge's bearing (was the run's FIRST edge), so a gently curved frontage (a few degrees per segment) or survey noise on a nominally-straight line groups into **ONE** side (one setback, one pill, one dimension) instead of many; a genuine corner still breaks the run, and a TIGHT curve (each segment turning beyond tolerance) correctly stays per-segment.

- **Recurrence: 2026-07-30 (×2) — owner, Weld County CO (breadcrumb `2221 E LAMAR BLVD STE 790 / Concept A`, Unincorporated, 40.348437 / -104.981121, ~62.7 ac, every edge 25 ft), with two screenshots.** The boundary follows a subdivision edge with a long curved run, so it carries dozens of short segments. One curved corner rendered **about twelve overlapping "25′" chips stacked into an unreadable pile**, and the whole parcel carried thirty-plus. Owner: *"the setbacks, that's way too many… this is completely ridiculous"*, and — the line that makes this a recurrence rather than a new number — *"I thought we had already fixed it."*
- **Why the ×1 fix could not hold, stated plainly.** B216 chained each edge to the PREVIOUS edge's bearing so a GENTLE curve groups into one side, and then **explicitly ruled that a TIGHT curve "correctly stays per-segment"** because each of those segments really is its own straight side. That is geometrically true and cartographically wrong: a filleted corner digitized as ~12 segments turns ~7.5° each — *just* past the ±7° tolerance — so every fillet segment became its own side, its own chip, and its own row in the panel. The ×1 verdict was the bug.
- **The fix — a separate, coarser grouping for the LABEL layer; `edgeRuns` is untouched.** New pure `lib/setbackChips.js` (`setbackChipRuns`) collapses **contiguous edges that share a setback VALUE** into one labelled run, breaking only where the run's **direction spread** — the spread of the running SIGNED heading since the run began — passes ~50°. Signed-and-spread (not a per-edge tolerance) is what makes all four real cases right at once: survey noise never accumulates, a filleted right-angle breaks exactly ONCE mid-fillet (a rounded rectangle reads as four sides), a hard corner breaks instantly as it always did, and a long arc breaks about every 50° of sweep, which is honest. The chip anchors at the **midpoint of the longest edge in its run** (the owner's rule) and that edge's length is its declutter priority.
- **Two screen-space guards on top, both re-decided every frame off the live zoom** (new shared `lib/screenDeclutter.js`): a chip whose anchor edge is shorter than ~26 px on screen is not drawn at all, and no two drawn chips may sit within ~40 px of each other — the lower-priority one drops, so the chip on the longest edge always survives a pile. Both are display-only: nothing is deleted, the dropped chip's edges keep their setback, and zooming in progressively reveals detail instead of painting everything at every scale. The SAME helper thins the parcel side-length dimension numbers (which piled up identically) and the vertex handles (B1185) — one declutter helper, reused, never a second one.
- **`edgeRuns.js` is deliberately NOT changed.** It still owns the ±7° geometric-SIDE model that drives run-length dimensions and B912 side resizing; every B214/B215/B216 test passes untouched. A filleted corner is a dozen geometric sides but ONE thing to label — the two models are different questions and now have different code.
