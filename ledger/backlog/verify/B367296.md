- **HIS REPORT.** Clay & Porter reads `Unincorporated / City of Houston ETJ`. Verbatim: *"it would be just City of Houston ETJ… like, it's either Unincorporated or it's COH ETJ."*
- **⛔ HE IS RIGHT ABOUT THE DISPLAY AND WRONG ABOUT THE REASON, AND THE CODE ENCODES THE RIGHT ONE.** The two are NOT mutually exclusive: in Texas an extraterritorial jurisdiction is BY DEFINITION the unincorporated band outside a city's corporate limits (Local Gov't Code ch. 42), so land in the COH ETJ is NECESSARILY unincorporated. The old label was **redundant, not wrong**. Making the two exclusive in the MODEL would be a real regression, and it was deliberately not done: `cityContainment: "none"` and `unincorporated: true` still hold on every ETJ site, and the detention / county tiers still read them. Only the PRESENTATION changed — once an ETJ is named, "Unincorporated" is implied and is not printed. Guarded by `test/jurisdictionLabel.test.js`'s "the MODEL is untouched" block.
- **⛔ THE ACTUAL DEFECT — ONE SEPARATOR MEANT TWO OPPOSITE THINGS.** `Clay & Porter: Unincorporated / City of Houston · ETJ` (Houston GOVERNS platting) sat in the same grammar as `Bain: Unincorporated / City of Katy · edge only` (Katy governs NOTHING — a polygon touching the parcel edge). A reader cannot tell them apart, and one mark must not mean both "this city regulates you" and "this city is next door".
- **THE FIX — ONE CANONICAL FORMATTER (`lib/jurisdictionLabel.js`), and a three-level grammar where each level means exactly one thing:** `·` joins GOVERNING slots, always leading with the governing authority (authority · ETJ · county · ISD) · `+` joins co-equal peers inside one slot (two cities, two counties, two districts) · `—` introduces the non-governing tail, and nothing after it regulates anything. `jurisdiction.js` keeps the DERIVATION and hands over a structured model; the strings live in one place.
