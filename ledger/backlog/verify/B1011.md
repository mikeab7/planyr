### B1011 — A tee that lands ON a bend in the through road was solved as if the through road were straight `[Site Planner / roadGeometry]` (bug) #site-planner #road  *(split out of B1010, 2026-07-25 — the remaining artifact at the owner's pond-west junction after B1010 cleaned the debris. Owner: "alright take care of that". Shipped 2026-07-25 with **V461**.)*
`[ ]` **⏳ merged — V461 PENDING (Verify: live — `Blocker: real-data`).** Root cause and fix as filed, plus a second defect in the same family found while verifying.
- **Root cause.** `teeGeometry` takes a SINGLE `throughDir`. When the tee node is also a bend vertex the through road has TWO tangents there (incoming and outgoing) and the caller could only hand over their bisector — so BOTH curb returns were built against a line the pavement never follows: one sat proud of the real edge (a thin dart), the other fell shy of it (a notch).
- **Fix — model a junction as N ARMS around a NODE.** New pure `nodeJunction({ node, arms, R })` in `roadGeometry.js`: each arm carries its OWN bearing, pavement half-width, available run and owning road id. Sort the arms by bearing; every ADJACENT PAIR is one corner and gets one curb-return fillet built against *that arm's own tangent*. The rule reproduces the straight tee (two armpits plus one ~180° gap that needs nothing) and subsumes the Y, the bend-with-branch and the 4-way without any arm being special. **Two arms of the SAME road are skipped** — that is the road's own bend, already joined by its polyline buffer, not a junction. `teeJunctionsOf` now builds [back, fwd, side] arms and maps the result into the existing `{ wedges, returns, throughTangents, nTee }` contract, so the dissolved-network render is unchanged.
- **Sub-foot tangent step (round 2).** A fillet is tangent to the arm's STRAIGHT edge line, but a real strip edge is the tessellated buffer of a polyline that may bend right there — so a wedge ending exactly at its tangent point left a visible step in the union outline. Each flank now OVERSHOOTS a little past the tangent and a hair INSIDE the edge, so the two boundaries cross instead of one stopping. Free, because everything inside the union is invisible; the inward bias is what guarantees it can never poke out.
- **A junction drawn with sub-foot slack was not recognised AT ALL (same family, found while verifying).** `TEE_COINCIDE_FT` was a flat 0.75 ft; on the owner's plan the 36′ aisle's endpoint sits **0.86 ft** from the 40′ aisle's vertex — a tenth of a foot over the line — so the app saw no tee, added no returns, and the two strips simply butted: a squared-off notch. Sub-foot slack is normal in hand-drawn geometry (grid snap, migration rounding, a nudged vertex) and is not a meaningful separation on a 40 ft road. The tolerance now scales with the narrower road's own width (`w/8`, floor 0.75 ft, cap 4 ft) — the same basis as B1010's debris rule — so two genuinely distinct vertices still can never be welded by accident. That junction now resolves with real returns.
