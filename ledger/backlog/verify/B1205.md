- **THE LOAD-BEARING RULE: filled AREA layers draw UNDER the site elements; LINE/stroke layers draw OVER them.** A contour crossing a building is a hairline and reads fine; a floodplain fill over a building buries it. That one distinction answers the complaint with no control at all — he sees his contours through his buildings, and his buildings never vanish under a blue wash. Neither Google Maps nor Apple Maps lets a user reorder layers; both use a fixed semantic hierarchy and expose opacity, which is exactly what shipped.
- **The model, in one place: `src/workspaces/site-planner/lib/mapStack.js`.** Bottom → top: basemap/aerial → GIS AREA layers → references → parcel → setback band → site elements → references explicitly promoted above the plan (B1198) → GIS LINE layers → labels and chips → manipulation handles. Every renderer names a tier from this file; nothing anywhere picks a z-index of its own.
- **Roles are DECLARED, never inferred at render time.** Every one of the 36 registered GIS sources carries an explicit `role: "area" | "line" | "point"` (points ride the over-the-plan band with the lines — burying a well or a hydrant under a pad defeats the point of turning it on). A source that publishes BOTH splits into its two roles via `roleLayers`: **FEMA** now paints its zone POLYGONS (sublayer 28) under the plan and its hazard BOUNDARIES (27) over it; **BKDD Drainage Information** splits sub-watersheds (116) from streams / BFE lines / outfalls (108, 121, 112, 2); **BKDD Master Plan** splits the study floodplains + proposed detention from the conveyance + proposed channel improvements. One panel row, one opacity, one status dot, two export requests.
- **The mechanism, and why it was not just a z-index.** Leaflet keeps every pane inside its own `_mapPane`, which carries the pan transform and therefore its own stacking context — so NO z-index on a pane can lift it above the planner SVG. The line-role pane is hosted OUTSIDE the map, in a sibling box above the SVG, with the two transforms that position it mirrored in the SAME statement as the original (VIEWPORT-STABLE): the wrap's live gesture transform and Leaflet's own map-pane translate. The host is `pointer-events: none`, so it can neither block a click nor steal a handle.
- **PDF-PARITY.** The sheet composites the same two bands: `under` at the backdrop anchor as before, `over` appended after the drawn plan and before the sheet furniture. Both raster and vector emitters take the band as an argument, so screen order and sheet order cannot drift.
- **NO new mode, NO keyboard shortcut, NO per-layer z-order UI** — asserted by a guard that fails if the Layers panel ever renders one.
- **RECONCILED, not competing, with the concurrent B1197/B1198 work** (merged to `main` mid-session; this branch was rebased onto it). B1198's References front/back stays exactly as shipped and now slots into this model: `lib/overlayOrder.js` owns the ordering WITHIN the reference bands, and its opt-in "Draw above the plan" band is a declared tier (`referenceFront`) rather than a second scheme. B1197's handle layer is the model's top tier.
- **⚠ ONE KNOWN DEVIATION, stated on the item as well as in the code.** `handle` is the top of the model, and B1197 made that true inside the plan (one `data-handle-layer` group, the SVG's last child, so a handle paints over and hit-tests ahead of every drawn element and every AREA layer). It does NOT paint over the LINE band, because that band is a separate DOM host above the whole SVG. Promoting the handle group into its own top host would move it out of the plan SVG, and **every planner drag relies on pointer moves BUBBLING to that SVG** — a handle in a sibling SVG would swallow the moves passing over it mid-drag. The deviation is bounded: the line band is thin and non-interactive, so a hairline may CROSS a handle but can never make one unreachable. Closing it means lifting the handle layer and its pointer plumbing together, with B1197's own guards — filed as **B1208**.
