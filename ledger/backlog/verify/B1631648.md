`[x]` **FIXED AND RED-PROOFED. Today's join rule, read before this fix, so the diagnosis is on the record:** `roadStripRing` (`siteGeometry.js`) built the pavement ring by calling `metesAndBounds.bufferPolyline(dense, width)`, which offsets each side of the (already-tessellated) centerline **independently**, per sparse control point, via a bisector-and-clamp approximation — `scale = min(1/max(0.3, cos(halfAngle)), 3)` — a flat ×3 ceiling on the miter length with **no bevel fallback** and **no check of the inside edge against the outside one**. Two compounding effects: (a) at the vertex itself, both sides get pushed out by the same clamped distance regardless of which side is convex vs. concave, so the inside overshoots past where the opposite edge sits; (b) because only the SPARSE points are offset and the output is connected by straight lines, a blown-out corner **tilts the whole adjoining leg's boundary**, so the width defect is not confined to the corner — it persists for most of the leg's length.

**MEASURED, not assumed — RED on unmodified `origin/main` (throwaway `git worktree`, not this branch):** a 40 ft road turning through the sweep asked for (150°/120°/90°/60°/45°/30°/15° interior angle) reads its correct 40 ft width at 150° through 45°, then **collapses to 32.84 ft at 30° and 20.51 ft at 15°**, measured 400 ft in from the vertex on a 2000 ft leg (well clear of any corner) — a 49% width loss holding for most of the leg's own length, not a localized corner artifact. This is the literal repro of "the corridor visibly stops holding its stated width through the bend."

**THE FIX.** `roadNetwork.roadSurfaceRing(pts, width)` — one robust `ClipperOffset` pass (`JoinType.jtMiter`, a stated `ROAD_JOIN_MITER_LIMIT = 2`, matching this repo's existing `pondOffset.js` convention) over the WHOLE open centerline at once, so the inside edge is resolved against the outside one rather than independently, with Clipper's own self-intersection repair handling compound cases (switchback, short segment) a per-vertex fix cannot. Past the miter limit the join automatically substitutes a flat BEVEL rather than continuing to spike — the "usual correct shape" the brief asked for, already this repo's engine for robust polygon ops (`pondOffset.js`, `roadNetwork.dissolveRings`), not a new dependency. `roadStripRing` calls it, falling back to the old `bufferPolyline` only on a degenerate/null result (never a silently dropped road surface). The curb-STROKE lines (`roadCurbLines`) get a parallel, lighter fix — `metesAndBounds.offsetPolylineMiterLimit`, a true per-vertex miter-with-bevel-fallback (real line-line intersection, not the old bisector approximation) — so the stroke tracks the now-correct pavement edge instead of visibly diverging from it at a sharp turn. `offsetPolyline`/`bufferPolyline` themselves are **untouched** (zero risk to easements, pipeline corridors, cross-section band lines, which still use them).

**RED-PROOF, `test/roadSurfaceJoin.test.js` (53 tests):** the angle sweep (150°→15°) × five widths (8/24/36/40/80 ft) asserts, on the FIX, both (a) the surface is a simple polygon (no self-intersection — checked pairwise over every non-adjacent edge) and (b) width measured perpendicular to each leg, at two points comfortably clear of the join's own transition zone (400 ft and 800 ft in, on a 2000 ft leg — this distance was MEASURED, not guessed: the transition zone's extent scales with width and angle, and 400 ft is proven stable for the sharpest/widest case in this sweep, 15°/80 ft), matches the stated width to within 0.1 ft. A dedicated sub-test runs the SAME sweep through the untouched `bufferPolyline` and proves it fails at the sharp end (the `brokenSharp` array is non-empty) — the suite would have failed on pre-fix `main`, not merely assumed to.

**ADJACENT CASES, each checked and reported (not just the sweep):**
- **Two-vertex straight road** — unchanged; a simple rectangle at full width, no join computed at all.
- **Gentle bend (170° interior)** — simple, holds width; was already fine on the old construction too (the defect is angle-dependent, not present at every bend).
- **Switchback (two 90° turns 30 ft apart, well under the road's own width)** — simple polygon; the two corners' bevel/miter geometry provably overlaps here and Clipper's own repair (not a per-vertex fix) is what makes this safe.
