**The report.** *"You've got setbacks by role, but what if their interpretation of the setbacks is different from your interpretation of the setbacks? We should be able to edit which one is the rear setback and which one is the side setback, just in case."* The inference is a good default and it is frequently wrong in the real world — a corner lot, a flag lot, a double-frontage lot, or simply a reviewer who reads the plat differently — and being wrong changes the required dimension.

**AUDIT-FIRST — what was actually there, and what was actually wrong.** B1191 already stored a user role in `pc.roles` and already let the *By side* picker set one, so "the user can correct it" was half-true. But `setRunRole` wrote a **DENSE** vector: correcting ONE run stamped **every** edge with whatever the app happened to infer at that moment. On the owner's fifteen-side parcel, one correction froze fourteen untouched sides against every later re-derivation — the exact opposite of derive-by-default. Nothing marked a role as the user's rather than the app's, and there was no way back to automatic.

**What shipped.**
- **A SPARSE override vector, `pc.roleOverrides`** — `null` at an edge means "still tracking the inference". This is the repo's established derive-by-default / preserve-once-touched pattern, now applied to roles. `resolveRoles` composes inference + overrides; `setRunOverride(…, role|null)` sets or clears a whole run.
- **Legacy plans migrate in place, without changing a pixel.** A dense `pc.roles` is narrowed on read to the entries that genuinely DIFFER from the inference — an entry equal to auto is a no-op either way, so nothing on screen moves, and everything the user never actually corrected goes back to tracking the boundary.
- **An override is never silently reset.** Alignment is enforced like `pc.setbacks`, and the two in-place reshapes that change the ring length now REMAP it: inserting a control point carries the role across the split (both halves are still the same side); deleting one merges two edges and the merged edge keeps the first half's role. Dragging a corner — the common reshape — never changed the length and always survived.
- **An overridden run is legible as overridden.** Its row reads **· yours** in the accent colour and carries `data-role-source="yours"`; an untouched row reads `"auto"`. The picker's top option is **"Automatic (Rear)"** — it both NAMES the role the app infers and is the way back to it. A **Roles: back to automatic** button appears next to Reset-to-default once anything is overridden, and disappears again when nothing is.
- **The role re-drives what the role feeds.** Changing it moves the run's edges into the other ordinance row, so the **By role** input for that role now writes them (and no longer writes the old one). *Stated plainly: this app has no external jurisdiction setback TABLE yet — the "required value for that role" is the number typed on the By-role row, and that is what follows the override.* One `pushHistory()` + one `setParcels` = one Ctrl-Z.
- **The NON-NEGOTIABLE still holds by construction:** a role is a LABEL, nothing here writes a setback VALUE, and the harness re-proves the drawn ring is identical across assignment and reassignment.
