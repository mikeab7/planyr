- **⛔ THE HISTORY IS THE FINDING. He reported this six times and it was "fixed" four times, correctly, every time.** Each earlier pass tested markup-against-markup, which already worked before all four of them. The variable was never the command — it is WHAT IS UNDERNEATH. Two markups in open land share a band, so "back" moves within it and the picture changes. A markup over a BUILDING is a question about the OTHER band.
- **THE MEASURED SYMPTOM, his words and our reproduction of them, verbatim from the pre-fix build on his real plan:**
  - Right-click a markup sitting over Building 1 → **Send to Back** → nothing visible happens. The markup still completely covers the building.
  - Re-open the menu → Send to Back and Send Backward are **greyed out**, hover reason `"This is the only one on the markups layer, so there is nothing to reorder it against."`
  - The command that does what he asked is **two rows below, under a different name** — "Send behind buildings".
- **⛔ WHY THAT IS A LOUD-FAILURE VIOLATION AND NOT A COPY PROBLEM.** The app succeeded at a DIFFERENT operation than the one that was asked for, and the only feedback it gave was a **disabled row**, which reads as "this is already done". A greyed control and a broken one are indistinguishable from the outside; a greyed control that is lying is worse than either. The stated reason made it worse rather than better: it names a "markups layer" the user has no way to know exists and cannot see.
- **THE DECISION, which the owner asked for a judgement on rather than compliance — his recommendation is ADOPTED, with one deliberate boundary.**
  - **Adopted:** for the three annotation families that carry a `behindEls` band (**markups · callouts / text boxes · measurements**) the ordered stack is `behind` then `above`, and the four Arrange modes address THAT stack. "Send to Back" crosses the band; "Send Backward" steps across the edge exactly one place; the two forward modes mirror it. Every drawing application the owner uses, Bluebeam included, means "behind everything under it", and now so does this one.
  - **"Send behind buildings" is KEPT, because it still has a distinct job** — it is the one-click named toggle in BOTH directions (its reverse, "Bring in front of buildings", is a way back that Arrange has no equivalent for), and for a plotted deed it carries the whole save-and-except group. It is no longer the ONLY way across, which was the defect.
  - **⛔ ELEMENTS ARE DELIBERATELY EXCLUDED, and this is an owner decision already on the record, not an oversight.** B316864 settled it: `road → paving → pond → parking → building` stays absolute for every untouched element, ordinary Arrange stops at the band edge, and one explicit "Force on top of everything" row is the only way across. The justification for the split is that an element's band is an ENGINEERING statement (pavement does not cover a building) that the drawing should keep by default, while an annotation's band is pure presentation — the user drew a rectangle and wants it under or over the plan, and no other meaning attaches. The element menu's no-op flash also already NAMES its band rule rather than going silent, so it is not the reported failure shape.
- **AND THE GREYING CAN NO LONGER CLAIM AN INVISIBLE SUCCESS.** A row is disabled only at a TRUE end of the whole stack, and it says so in the terms the user is thinking in — `"Already behind everything on the plan."` A lone markup over a building is no longer greyed at all, because it genuinely has somewhere to go.
- **THE FIX.** `lib/arrange.js` gains `arrangeAcrossBands` (the pure four-mode rule over both bands, returning the same minimal patch plus an explicit `cross`) and `arrangeBandFlags` (the band-aware greying). `SitePlanner.jsx`'s `arrangeSel` routes all three annotation families through it via one new `annotationFamily` resolver — every place that used to re-derive the two-band model per family is where the four earlier fixes drifted apart. `setMarkupBand` was lifted out of the right-click menu's closure so it has a name and more than one caller, and it gained the re-stack its two siblings always had (the inline version flipped the flag and left `z` alone, so a markup brought back in front could land under one it used to be above).
