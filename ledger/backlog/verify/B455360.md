**WHAT WAS ACTUALLY WRONG, and it is worse than "the message is unhelpful".** Two splitters shipped, and between them they covered only trivial cuts. A 2-point cut went to `splitPolygonByLine`, which is correct. Anything with a bend went to `splitPolygonByPath`, which projected the first and last cut vertex onto the NEAREST polygon edge, refused outright when both landed on the same edge, and then emitted exactly TWO rings by walking the boundary between them. Three consequences: it could not represent a cut crossing the boundary more than twice (so three or more pieces was unreachable BY CONSTRUCTION); it never checked that the cut stayed inside the lot; and on a concave lot the two rings overlapped or omitted a wedge, at which point a downstream area guard refused the whole thing with `That cut crosses the parcel ambiguously (concave shape) — try a straight cut between two opposite edges.` **That refusal is measured, not recalled**: `test/polygonSplit.test.js` runs the pre-fix pipeline verbatim and reproduces it on his Goose Creek and Bain tracts.

**THE FIX IS ONE ENGINE WITH NO CASE ANALYSIS.** `splitPolygonByCut` builds the PLANAR ARRANGEMENT of the boundary and the cut and enumerates its faces: node every intersection into a shared tolerance-snapping registry, emit boundary sub-edges plus the cut sub-edges whose midpoints are strictly inside the lot, prune degree-1 nodes, trace faces, keep the ones that are land. Both readings of "more complicated" fall out of it rather than being handled — a multi-segment cut, and a cut crossing the boundary 2k times giving k+1 pieces — as do a cut that re-enters and a cut that closes a loop inside the lot. There is no crossing-count branch anywhere, which is the point.

**⛔ TWO CONDITIONS IN HIS REAL RECORDED PARCELS BROKE THE FIRST DRAFT, AND NEITHER IS A TEXTBOOK POLYGON. Do not "simplify" either guard away.**
1. **Goose Creek's ring is PINCHED.** It runs out to a point, clockwise around an interior exclusion while the rest of the lot runs counter-clockwise, and back through that same point — a hole reached by a zero-width slit. Under an even-odd containment test that hole reads as LAND and the split invents ~13,400 sf the parcel does not own. Faces are therefore classified by the WINDING rule, which is also what the shoelace acreage the whole app quotes already assumes; getting the two to agree is what makes "the pieces sum to the acreage" a real check rather than a coincidence. Mutation-proven: keep every bounded face and the two Goose Creek tests go red.
2. **Bain's ring self-overlaps.** It runs 1,296 ft out along a zero-width prong and back, and the returning leg clips the outgoing one about two tenths of an inch above its base. Left un-noded, that hairline crossing has no node and the face walk runs straight through it, so the boundary is now noded against ITSELF before anything else. Mutation-proven: drop that pass and the Bain test goes red. Its consequence is also handled rather than hidden — the shoelace figure counts an 8 sf crumb twice, so the pieces cannot sum to the STATED acreage, and rather than refuse his largest parcel over 8 sf on 109 acres the engine reports `outlineDrift` and the app says so on screen.

**LOUD-FAILURE, in four places.** Nothing is silent. (a) Every refusal names what is wrong with THAT cut — never crosses the parcel · runs along the property line · stops inside the parcel · doesn't separate it · the pieces don't add up · the parcel's own outline crosses itself — and a unit test fails if any refusal message is generic, duplicated, or contains the retired "try a straight cut" advice. (b) A cut producing three or more pieces SAYS how many. (c) A scrap under a hundred-thousandth of the parent is not made into a parcel, and its count and area are reported. (d) A parcel whose own outline overlaps itself is named, with the discrepancy in square feet. The one hard refusal that remains is a genuine area mismatch with no crossing to explain it: nothing is written.

**ATTRIBUTES, and what is RECOMPUTED rather than copied.** Address, account and the attribute bag ride onto every piece. Acreage and the badge were already derived from `points` at render, so they are right for free. The per-edge vectors are the ones that would have gone stale invisibly — a piece has different edges from its parent — so `setbacks`, `roleOverrides` and the legacy dense `roles` are REMAPPED through the engine's own edge provenance (`edgeSrc`: for each edge of the new ring, the parent edge index it came from, or -1 for an edge the cut created), and a cut edge takes the plan's default setback and no role assignment rather than a neighbour's value. `label` is deliberately NOT carried — see the owner questions.

**HOLES.** The parcel model is single-ring (`pc.points`), so there are none to preserve today; the pinched-hole case above is the nearest thing the data actually contains and it is handled. The engine is ring-topological rather than boundary-walking, so real interior rings would be fed into the same arrangement — not a rewrite.
