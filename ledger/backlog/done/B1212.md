**The report.** *"It's annoying if I have a building already there because now I can't edit it because it's behind the building. If I'm already doing the work of clicking on the parcel, then the options to edit the setbacks should be above the elements."* On his Weld plan the buildings sit hard against the setback line, so the chips and the lot's grab band land under a building's fill and the click never reaches them.

**Cause.** Paint order. The chips and the setback-ring grab band were emitted in the parcel band of the canvas — **before** the element pass — so anything drawn later covered them. (The parcel's vertex handles were already in the selection-chrome layer, which is why *those* always worked; the inconsistency is what made this read as arbitrary.)

**The fix.** Both move into the existing `data-export="skip"` **selection-chrome group** at the end of the canvas, and only for the **SELECTED** parcel: selection is the signal of intent, so the lot's controls win for exactly as long as it is selected. Order inside the group is deliberate — grab band first, then the chips (so a chip keeps its own click target over the band), then the vertex handles last (the smallest targets are never covered). Every other parcel keeps its grab band exactly where it was.
- **Only the INTERACTIVE chrome moved.** The setback ring, its casing, the parcel outline and the parcel fill all stay in the parcel band, so a selected lot can never hide a building.
- **The export path is unaffected**, and this is asserted rather than assumed: the chips still carry `data-export="skip"` and the group they moved into is itself `data-export="skip"`, so `exportSheet`'s one `querySelectorAll('[data-export="skip"]').remove()` still strips them.
- **Stated trade-off:** while a lot is selected, a press within the grab band along its setback ring moves the LOT even where a building is painted there. That is the behaviour asked for, it is scoped to the selected lot, and deselecting restores the old order.

> **Tests / verification.** `test/setbackChipQuiet.test.js` source guards — the chips and the band render after the element pass, inside the chrome group, in that order, with the handles last; the ring / casing / outline are still BEFORE the chrome group; the export-skip attribute survives the move. Headless: with a building deliberately covering the whole interior, **8 of 8 chips sit over the building**, every one is the topmost element at its own centre, and clicking one opens its inline editor.
