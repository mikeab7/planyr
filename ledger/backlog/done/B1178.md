- **HOW IT WAS FOUND, and why it is not a false alarm.** The B1173–B1177 dispatch measured +15.8 / +24.3 / +17.3 KB and that looked far too large for ~2 KB of source. Attributed by stashing only `src/` and rebuilding: **`main` on its own accounts for +13.9 / +22.4 / +16.9**, and the dispatch's actual cost is **+1.9 KB site route, +1.8 KB total, +0.4 KB largest chunk** — which is the right order for what it changed. The gate PASSED in both cases (everything stayed inside the band), so nothing was suppressed and no ceiling was touched.
- **WHY IT MATTERS.** The band exists to absorb a feature's honest growth. If a fixed cost is already eating it, the next few features trip a ceiling for reasons that have nothing to do with them, and the reader is told to "pay it back with an optimization" for bytes they did not add. It also makes the byte-attribution B1146 added misleading unless every reader knows to stash-and-compare first.
- **WHAT IS NOT YET KNOWN — this is the part that needs the work.** `package-lock.json` IS committed, so the obvious explanation (caret ranges floating on a fresh `npm install`) should not apply, and that has NOT been confirmed either way. The plausible causes, in order: the recorded baselines were captured on a different Node/npm/rollup than a fresh clone resolves to; an optional/platform-specific dependency differs; or a baseline was recorded from an incremental rather than a clean `dist/`.
- **SUGGESTED SHAPE, not a decision:** first establish whether a clean clone at the baseline's own commit reproduces the baseline. If it does not, the fix is to make the measurement environment part of the record (capture the Node + npm version alongside each baseline, and re-baseline from a clean build) rather than to move any number. **Do not "fix" this by raising or re-recording a baseline until the cause is known** — that would launder the drift into the record permanently.
