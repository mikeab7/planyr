- **WHY IT EXISTS.** A LIVE layer needs no vintage — whatever it draws is whatever FEMA is serving this second. The moment the picture comes out of a file we built, *"when was this true?"* becomes a question the panel has to be able to answer. **A fast map that is quietly six months stale is worse than a slow one that is right, because nothing about it looks wrong.**
- **WHERE IT RENDERS, and why there.** As a chip on the `REGULATORY` tier heading the FEMA row already sits under — **PANEL-BREVITY rule 3, a named state rather than a sentence explaining the state.** It therefore costs the default view **zero visible lines** (measured: `node ui-audit/panel-copy-budget.mjs --check` green before and after, every region within budget), and nothing had to be removed to pay for it. It is shown ONLY when tiles are the source; a live layer's vintage is "now" and stamping it would be noise.
- **⛔ IT CANNOT DISAPPEAR — that is the whole design.** `floodVintageStamp` returns `{ text, known, date }` and never null: a missing manifest, a malformed one, a county with no row, an empty date, or a date that is not `YYYY-MM-DD` all produce **"NFHL vintage unknown — this county's tiles carry no effective date"** in the warn colour. Omitting the line on a failed fetch is exactly the silent-absence failure B1093 exists to prevent, and the test asserts all eight failure shapes produce a non-empty "unknown".
- **THE DATE IS SPELLED, NOT SLASHED.** `Nov 15, 2019`, not `11/15/2019` — a FIRM effective date is a legal fact, and a slashed numeric date reads as a US date to half the world and a day-first date to the other half. The county key is the SITE's own (the same one the archive is picked with), so the panel can never stamp one county's date over another county's map.
- **GUARD.** `test/floodTiles.test.js` — the stamp read out of the REAL committed manifest, every unknown-shape, the mixed-case county key (B298403), and the month-name formatting. Headless: `verify-flood-tiles.mjs` asserts the chip is on screen with tiles AND absent with the flag off.
