### B2478963 — Map/Comps site-plan overlays carry no state or county, so their zone comes from the coarse point resolver `[shared/overlay]` (task) #site-planner #coordinates  *(filed 2026-10-29 from B2478960. **DEDUPE-FIRST** — net new: the owner's brief said the point-envelope fallback "stays coarse and flagged"; this is the measured size of that coarseness and the cheap way to retire it.)*
`[x]` **DONE — closed by B2494336 (2026-10-29), by deriving the county from the anchor point at read time rather than threading `state`/`county` through.** Overlay rows (`site_plan_overlays`) carry no county; threading one in would add a stored COPY of a shared fact that can go stale when an overlay is dragged across a county line (the all-shared-data-has-one-source rule), through ~10 call sites. A point-in-county answer needs no stored field and no migration, and cannot disagree with the geometry. Residual, said plainly: before the county polygons are resident (first moments of a Site-route load) a bare point still gets the coarse flagged answer, and within ~150 m of a county line the simplified polygons can pick either side (`nearEdge:true`). *Original text follows.* `overlayPlacement.placementZone(lat, lon)` has only the placement's anchor, so it uses `statePlane.zoneForPoint`. Measured over the 3,144 county bbox-centres: **89.7% exact zone, 3.2% a neighbouring zone of the right state, 7.2% a neighbouring state's zone** (worst near state lines). A wrong neighbour changes scale by parts in 10⁴ and the convergence by a fraction of a degree; the placement stays self-consistent (forward and inverse use the same zone) but is not the legally defined zone.
- Verify: sandbox
- **Fix:** carry `state` + `county` (the comp's / site's, already known where the overlay is attached) into `placementZone` and pass them to `resolveZone` — the county answer always beats the box. A stored `site_plan_overlays` row would need no migration (derive at read). Also consider a stored per-overlay `zoneId` stamp so the zone cannot flip when the overlay is dragged across a coarse boundary.
