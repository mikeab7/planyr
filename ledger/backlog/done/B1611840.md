`[x]` **FIXED AND RED-PROOFED.** Every road surface ring emitted by `dissolveRings` (`roadNetwork.js` — the ONE choke point every road-to-pad (`teeGeometry`) AND road-to-road (`nodeJunction`) junction dissolve passes through) could carry a spurious near-duplicate vertex right at a curb-return tangent point: two pieces from different generators (a tessellated strip, an analytically-solved fillet wedge, occasionally the defensive convex-hull fallback in `teeGeometry`'s wedge builder) rarely land on exactly the same point, and `dissolveRings`'s own morphological close (which exists to bridge that sub-inch gap) — or the fallback hull itself — could leave a short "in and out" detour of a few hundredths to a couple of feet, producing a turn-angle spike of roughly ±100–135° between two very short consecutive segments.

Confirmed GREEN after the fix.

**THE FIX (`roadNetwork.js`):** `ringTurnAngles`/`ringSpikes`/`collapseRingSpikes` (new, pure, exported). A vertex is a spurious spike only when BOTH the turn there exceeds `RING_SPIKE_TURN_DEG` (100°) AND at least one adjoining segment is under `RING_SPIKE_LEN_FT` (2.0 ft) — a smoothly tessellated fillet arc (`DEFAULT_TESS_DEG` per vertex, `roadGeometry.js`) never turns anywhere near that much per vertex regardless of how short its chords get at a tight radius, so this can never eat real curvature; every genuine sharp corner in this geometry (a road's own flat end cap, ~90°; the mouth chord between two curb returns meeting near-perpendicular, close to 180°) rides a segment of real length (a couple of feet or more), so it is never caught either — confirmed directly: the 2.49 ft+ legitimate mouth-chord backing segments found during the sweep survive the cleanup untouched. `collapseRingSpikes` is applied to `outer` and every hole at all three ring-emission points in `dissolveRings` (the single-input early return, the PolyTree walk, and nowhere in the degraded catch-fallback, which stays untouched on purpose). Iteratively removes one spike per pass (re-measuring afterward, since a removal can expose a new short segment between its former neighbours), bounded by a `ring.length + 8` guard so it can never run long, and never drops a ring below a triangle.

**Area impact measured, not assumed:** across the full angle sweep on both families, the cleanup changed a dissolved region's area by at most ~7 sq ft out of several thousand (always ≤0, i.e. only ever closing a spurious sliver, never opening one) — sub-inch at the vertex level, imperceptible at working zoom (PERCEPTUAL-PARITY), matching "nothing visible today."
