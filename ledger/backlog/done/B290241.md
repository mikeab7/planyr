Minted from this branch's reserved block B290240–B290255 against origin/main d36f98d. **DEDUPE-FIRST** — searched `gridConvergence`, `deedAlign`, `basis of bearings`, `B625`, `B1106`, `B1109`, `B1112`: B625 owns the empirical fit's rotational alias, B1106/B1112 own the grid-vs-ground factor. Nobody had ever asked which ZONE the convergence came from. New item.)*
`[x]` `deedAlign.gridConvergenceDeg` computed the basis-of-bearings correction from the hardcoded EPSG:2278 Texas South Central cone at every point on earth.
- Verify: sandbox — **VERIFIED on the real screen 2026-08-09** (`ui-audit/verify-colorado-deed-north.mjs`, 14/14).
- **The defect.** Convergence is γ = n·(λ − λ₀) — a function of the ZONE's cone constant and central meridian, i.e. of exactly the two things that change when you leave the zone. Measured at the owner's Johnstown ground: the Texas cone answered **−2.885°** where Colorado North (2231) answers **+0.378°**. A 3.26° error, rotating a plotted deed **75 ft off across a 1,320 ft boundary run** — and the app announced it in a toast as *"rotated 2.89° counter-clockwise for the State Plane grid convergence here"*. Every Colorado county measured the same way (Denver −2.923 vs +0.320 · Fort Collins −2.967 vs +0.270 · Colorado Springs −2.713 vs +0.590), and Chicago ground turned by **5.55°**.
- **The fix.** `resolveZone({state, county, lat, lon})` — county first (Colorado's zone boundaries ARE county lines), the coarse point envelope second — then the convergence in THAT zone. Texas resolves to `tx_sc`, whose generic Lambert engine reproduces `shared/coordinates/index.js` bit-for-bit, so Texas is unchanged by construction (asserted with a 10-dp equality against the pre-fix implementation inlined in the test). This makes `shared/coordinates/statePlane.js` its first production consumer; it rides the lazy deed chunk (`deedLazy.js`), never the boot path.
- **CONTRACT CHANGE, deliberate:** unresolvable input now returns **null**, not 0. 0 is a real answer here ("you are on the central meridian, nothing to correct") and the caller ROTATES A SURVEYED BOUNDARY by what it returns; the old sentinel collided with the meaningful value and `SitePlanner`'s `Math.abs(conv) < 0.01` read one as the other. The caller gained its own branch and a named message, and refuses to rotate.
- **On screen** (real browser, no sign-in, no GIS): Johnstown turns **+0.3784°** and says *"rotated 0.38° clockwise"*; Denver **+0.3204°**; Katy **+1.5598°**, unchanged; Chicago turns **0.000000°** and says *"Planyr doesn't carry a State Plane zone for this location — so there is no grid rotation it can compute honestly."*
