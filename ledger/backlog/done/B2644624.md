- **What was measured on `main` (headless Chromium, real `page.mouse.wheel`, a per-frame in-page recorder — `ui-audit/verify-schedule-scroll-smooth.mjs`, 15 failures on `main`):**
  1. **Rows pop in late.** The row window was re-derived from the raw scrollTop and held only ~100 px of spare rows below the fold, so one wheel notch out-ran the React commit: in 28–80 of ~170–190 frames the bottom row of a pane had NOTHING mounted (Grid, Gantt and Split, scrolling down).
  2. **A full re-render per row boundary.** 8–30 row-list commits per screenful per pane (30 on small trackpad-style deltas), and 50–213 ms main-thread tasks per scroll step at 4× CPU throttle — every scroll event called `setScrollTop(raw)`.
  3. **A blocking wheel listener.** `window.addEventListener("wheel", …, {passive:false})` (the Ctrl+wheel zoom) was registered permanently, so every plain wheel notch had to wait for the main thread before the browser may scroll — a busy frame (1) stalls the scroll itself.
  4. **The Split scroll sync wrote a STALE position back into the pane being scrolled.** The rAF-released `lock` could let the other pane's echo through one frame late. Reproduced deterministically by driving the pane 30 px per frame (what a smoothed wheel does): it ended at 3370 instead of 3400, i.e. the scroll was pulled back and stopped short. Also meant every second event of the driven pane was ignored and the Gantt trailed a frame behind.
- **The fix, per cause:** (1)+(2) the window now moves in CHUNKS of 8 rows with 12 rows of overscan each side (`WIN_CHUNK`/`WIN_OVER`/`rowWindowTop`/`rowWindow`) — React state changes only when the scroll crosses a chunk, ~4 commits per screenful per pane, and the next rows are already mounted; (3) the zoom listener is registered only while Ctrl/Cmd is down (keydown/keyup/blur) and Ctrl+wheel still zooms; (4) the sync remembers the exact value it wrote and drops only that echo (`echo` ref), so nothing is ever ignored or rewound.
- **Result:** the same harness on the fixed build: 0 uncovered frames, 3.6–4.5 commits per screenful per pane, no blocking listener at rest, driven pane lands exactly where driven (down 3400, up to 0), panes level in every frame. Also checked: grid zoom 0.5×/2× (Ctrl+wheel) and a selected row scrolled away and back — 0 unmounted sample points; mobile WebKit (iPhone 13 profile, panes stacked): 0 holes and no backward frame driving scrollTop per frame (mobile WebKit has no wheel API, so real touch feel is not covered).
- **Not reproduced (said loudly, per the brief):** the top-edge "resistance" itself. In headless Chromium every wheel step moves the full commanded distance right up to the top and bottom edge and lands exactly on 0 / max on `main` as well as after the fix (Split, Grid, Gantt; notch and single 4000 px flick), and frame cost near the top is the LOWEST of any stretch. A headless browser has no wheel smoothing and no compositor thread, so it cannot feel a held-back scroll. What the fix removes is everything found that can hold a scroll back in a real browser (items 3 and 4 above, and the main-thread load of item 2). Whether that is the whole of what he felt is his call.
