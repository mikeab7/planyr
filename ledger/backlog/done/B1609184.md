`[x]` **IMPLEMENTED AND VERIFIED THIS SESSION.** Reproduced before fixing, at the owner's own window: growing from the top scrolls `note-mat` (that is the compensation that makes the bottom edge hold, and the owner has verified that half as correct), and a 60px grow put the page's top edge at screen y **90** against a mat whose visible area starts at **126** — so the edge just dragged, and the 14px grip straddling it (84–98), were outside the scroller's own visible box. `document.elementFromPoint` at the grip's centre answered something else entirely: ungrabbable without scrolling back by hand.

**Fix: `scrollToReach` (`lib/notesPageHeight.js`), pure, applied once at RELEASE.** It gives back the LEAST scroll that brings the whole grip back inside the visible area, and exactly nothing when it is already there — so a drag that ends in view never moves the picture at all, and a top-edge SHRINK (which moves the edge down, never up) never triggers it. ⛔ The visible area is the INTERSECTION of the scroller and the window, never the scroller's own rect: at the owner's short window `note-mat`'s own element runs past the bottom of the screen (measured 126 → 606 in a 465-tall window), so its rect alone is not what a person can see. ⛔ It runs at release and never mid-gesture, deliberately: the drag itself has to stay 1:1 with the pointer, which is the half the owner verified as correct and the brief told us not to re-derive; "the edge you are dragging stays reachable when the drag ends" is his own wording and is exactly the promise kept. The trade-off is stated rather than hidden — a grow that dragged the edge far out of view ends with a correspondingly large scroll correction, because the correction is defined as how far out of view the edge went. The alternative (capping how far the edge may travel during the drag) would have changed the drag behaviour he confirmed as correct.
