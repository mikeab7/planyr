### V455792 — B817552: the street basemap no longer shows CARTO's "API KEY REQUIRED" watermark, on a real device
**What was verified here, extensively, without a real device pass.** Real headless Chromium (Playwright) driving the live dev-server app with network-request interception confirmed: zero requests to any `cartocdn.com` host fire for the street layer; the requested Esri tile URLs use the correct `{z}/{y}/{x}` axis order (y before x — the exact mistake that broke the satellite toggle the first time it was built); the satellite toggle works both directions (street → satellite → street) with zero console errors; the attribution control reads the corrected Esri-only credit text with no OpenStreetMap wording left over. Tile *pixels* could not be painted inside this sandbox — the egress proxy allows the HTTP request through but the image bytes don't fully decode for the browser to render, so the map area itself renders grey in the captured screenshots even though the underlying requests are provably correct. The pin-legibility comparison between `World_Topo_Map` and `World_Street_Map` that decided the pick was done separately, outside the live app, by downloading real tiles for both services at a dense downtown-Houston view and overlaying synthetic pins across the app's real rating-colour ramp.
**What is NOT proven, and why it parks here.** Nobody has loaded `planyr.io` on a real device/browser and looked at the actual rendered tiles — that's the only way to confirm the watermark is truly gone (not just that the request no longer targets CARTO) and that pins stay legible against the real rendered topo basemap at the owner's own working zoom.

1. Cache-busted load, `planyr.io/?cb=<any value>#/food`, street basemap, at several zooms including a dense area (e.g. downtown/inner-loop Houston). **Expect:** no "API KEY REQUIRED" text anywhere on the map, at any zoom — the defect this item exists to fix.
2. Toggle to satellite, then back to street, a few times. **Expect:** both directions work cleanly; no blank/grey tiles, no stuck loading state, no console errors.
3. With pins on screen over a dense cluster of restaurants, look at legibility. **Expect:** pin colours (the 1–10 rating ramp, reds through greens) read clearly against the muted grey/white roads and light terrain colours of the new basemap — nothing competing with a saturated road colour.
4. Check the attribution control (bottom corner / the credit line). **Expect:** credits Esri (and HERE/Garmin/the GIS community) only — no OpenStreetMap text remains anywhere.
