Privacy

Planyr is built by Michael, an industrial real-estate developer in Texas, for people doing site work — developers, civil engineers, general contractors, architects and brokers. This describes what the software collects, where it lives, who can see it, and how you can have it removed. It's written to match what the code actually does, not a template — if that changes, this page changes with it.

What Planyr collects

  • Account details. Your email address and password, handled by Planyr's authentication provider (Supabase). If you add a name or organization to your profile, that's stored too. Nothing else is required to create an account.
  • Your content. The sites, layouts, drawings, comps, notes and schedules you build inside Planyr — the working records the software exists to hold — stored as structured data in Planyr's database.
  • Files you upload. Drawings and surveys you add to Review or the Library — PDF, DWG, DXF and image files — are stored as files in a private Google Drive account that Planyr itself controls, organized into a folder for your account. This is not your personal Drive: the connection is limited to a narrow permission (Google calls it "drive.file") that only reaches files Planyr itself created, so Planyr can never see or touch anything else in your own Google account.
  • Diagnostic notes. If the software hits an error, or you use the "that felt slow just now" report control, a short technical note is recorded — which part of the app, a build identifier, your browser type, the page address, and an error message. It never includes your drawings, your files, or your account details.
  • Problem reports. If you use the report-a-problem control, Planyr records the page you were on, a build identifier, your screen size and browser, a de-identified project reference, and which map layers were turned on — never your drawing geometry or file names, and never your email address if you were signed out when you sent it.

Where it lives

  • The database and sign-in system: Supabase, a hosted Postgres database (Postgres is the database engine — think of it as the filing cabinet everything else reads from), running on Amazon Web Services in the United States.
  • Uploaded files: a private Google Drive account Planyr controls, described above.
  • Hosting: Cloudflare Pages serves the web pages you load, and a small number of Planyr-written server functions running on Cloudflare relay your uploads and downloads between your browser and Drive — they pass the bytes through without keeping their own copy.

Who can see it

  • Nobody but you, by default. Every record in the database carries a rule (Postgres calls this row-level security) that only lets your own signed-in account read or write it. There's no shared or team view today, and if one is ever added, turning it on will always be something you choose, never something that happens on its own.
  • Michael, who runs Planyr, has an internal operations view over things like the diagnostic notes above and feature requests people submit — never a view into another account's sites, drawings or files.
  • Nobody else. Planyr does not sell, rent, or share your content with advertisers or data brokers.

What leaves your browser

When you search for or open a site, Planyr looks up public records from government mapping services — county and city GIS (Geographic Information System — a mapping server that answers "what's at this location") servers, FEMA flood data, USGS elevation and terrain data, NOAA rainfall records, USDA soil surveys, and Esri's and OpenStreetMap's address-lookup services — to pull in things like parcel boundaries, flood zones and terrain for the location you're working in. Only the location you're looking at is sent to these services — never your name, email or account details. If you add your own Mapillary API key, Planyr can also request street-level imagery from Mapillary using that same location.

When you create an account, Cloudflare's Turnstile check runs to confirm you're not an automated script signing up — the same company that hosts the app, and it doesn't track you across other sites.

Planyr does not use advertising networks, analytics trackers, or tracking pixels of any kind, and nothing here uses third-party cookies.

Cookies and local storage

Planyr does not use cookies. It uses your browser's own local storage to remember things like your sign-in session and your display preferences — that stays on your device and is never sent anywhere else.

Security

Every connection to Planyr runs over HTTPS. The database enforces the same per-account access rule described above at the database level, not just in the app's own code, and every third-party key or secret that could act on your behalf stays on Planyr's servers — none of them are ever shipped to your browser.

How long things are kept

Crash and error notes (the diagnostic notes above) are deleted automatically ninety days after they're written, except for a note you send yourself through the "that felt slow just now" control, which is kept for a year since it's the rarest and most useful kind. Problem reports you submit are kept until reviewed; there's no separate automatic-deletion schedule for those today. Your own content — your sites, drawings, comps, notes and uploaded files — is kept for as long as your account exists, or until you ask Planyr to remove it.

Deleting your account and your data

There's no self-serve delete-my-account control yet. Email support@planyr.io and your account, your saved sites and drawings, your uploaded files, and your profile will be removed.

Children

Planyr is a professional tool for real-estate and construction work. It isn't directed at children, and Planyr doesn't knowingly collect information from anyone under 13.

Changes

If what Planyr collects or how it's handled changes, this page changes with it — the date at the top will move.
